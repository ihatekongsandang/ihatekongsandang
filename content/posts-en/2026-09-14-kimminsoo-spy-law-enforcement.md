## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It announces that, thanks to the public's support, the amended espionage law takes effect this month, so that leaking state secrets not only to North Korea but also to foreign countries will be punished as espionage.
- It then says, "I wonder whether there is anyone left in South Korea to catch spies," and vows to fix the collapsed security system.

## What reports confirm

- An amendment to the Criminal Act expanding the scope of the crime of espionage from "enemy states" to "foreign countries or equivalent organizations" reportedly passed a plenary session of the National Assembly on Feb. 26, 2026, was promulgated on March 12 and took effect on Sept. 13. Espionage for a foreign country or the like carries a prison term of three years or more, while espionage for an enemy state carries, as before, death, life imprisonment or a prison term of seven years or more.
- The push for the amendment reportedly stemmed from cases in which the crime of espionage could not be applied when secrets were passed to a country other than North Korea, such as the 2024 case in which a civilian employee of the Defense Intelligence Command handed agents' personal information to a Chinese national.
- Ahead of the law taking effect, several experts reportedly expressed concern over whether the police have the capacity to lead espionage investigations now that the NIS's anti-communist investigative authority has been abolished and counterintelligence functions have been dispersed.

## What we left out of the summary

- The expression that "there is no one left to catch spies" is supreme council member Kim Min-soo (김민수)'s assessment, and we found no report confirming in figures the current size of the espionage investigation workforce.
