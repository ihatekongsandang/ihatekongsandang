This post is based on the original post's text and image. Related post (may be available only in Korean): [Kim Kye-ri (김계리) and the controversy over a North Korean POW's move to South Korea](/post/2026-09-25-lawyer-nk-pow-ukraine).

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says two North Koreans who had expressed intent to defect were forcibly repatriated to North Korea on the Moon Jae-in (문재인) administration's orders, pointing out that under the Constitution, North Korean defectors are citizens of South Korea.
- It states that those forcibly repatriated were beheaded.

## What reports confirm

- In November 2019, the government reportedly sent two North Korean fishermen who had expressed intent to defect back to North Korea through Panmunjom. The government at the time explained that they were vicious criminals who had killed 16 fellow crew members and were therefore not subject to protection.
- The Seoul Central District Court reportedly deferred sentencing (선고유예) in February 2025 on prison terms of 10 months for former National Security Office chief Chung Eui-yong (정의용) and former NIS director Suh Hoon (서훈), and 6 months for former presidential chief of staff Roh Young-min (노영민) and former Unification Minister Kim Yeon-chul (김연철), all indicted on charges including violating the National Intelligence Service Act. The court reportedly acknowledged the charges but withheld pronouncing the sentences.
- Prosecutors reportedly sought five years in prison for former chief Chung and former director Suh, four years for former chief of staff Roh, and three years for former minister Kim at the appellate trial in June 2026, with the appellate ruling reportedly scheduled for Oct. 21. In September, 41 defector organizations reportedly called for severe punishment at the appellate trial.

## What we left out of the summary

- The claim that the repatriated fishermen were "beheaded" was not treated as fact, as there is no report confirmed by the government or a credible institution.
