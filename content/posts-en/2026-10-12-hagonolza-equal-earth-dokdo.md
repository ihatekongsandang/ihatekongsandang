This post is based on the original caption and its 5 images. The original reposts a card-news series from another account (@oneul.right). Because the first image prominently features a photo of a person, we used a card made by this site.

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says Dokdo disappeared from the Korean edition of the "Equal Earth" world map, and that while the mapmaker said this resulted from consultations with Korean and Japanese embassy officials, the foreign ministry denied having agreed to or reached any deal on the deletion.
- It says Lee Ki-in (이기인), chair of the Reform Party's emergency committee, pushed back on social media, saying "if the South Korean government agreed to delete Dokdo, that is grounds for impeachment," and demanded that the presidential office disclose records of who made contact, when, and what views were conveyed. It also quotes remarks such as "How can erasing our land from a map be a 'compromise'?" and "A change of government does not change the responsibility to defend our territory."
- It sums up the core of the dispute as the mapmaker citing consultations while the foreign ministry denies any consultation, adding that whether the South Korean government agreed to the deletion has not been confirmed.

## What reports confirm

- On Oct. 8, the cyber diplomacy group VANK said that in the Korean-language world map it made in November 2025 reflecting the East Sea, Ulleungdo and Dokdo, and that was officially listed on the Equal Earth website in June 2026, the East Sea label remained but Dokdo had been dropped; it lodged a formal protest and demanded restoration, it was reported. Dokdo was shown on the map on the official website on Sept. 6 but was gone from the map on Oct. 8, according to reports.
- In an email to VANK, the Equal Earth side reportedly explained that it deleted Dokdo after consulting Korean and Japanese embassy officials in Washington, D.C., describing removal of Dokdo from both the Korean and Japanese editions as a kind of compromise.
- In an Oct. 9 statement, the foreign ministry reportedly said "claims that South Korea agreed with Japan to delete Dokdo from each country's language edition, or agreed to delete Dokdo from the map, are entirely untrue." The ministry said it asked the mapmaker about the circumstances through the relevant diplomatic mission and that the mapmaker replied to the effect that it was "a measure considering fairness between both sides"; it also said that, as far as it knows, Dokdo has never been labeled on the Japanese edition of Equal Earth. The ministry said it would keep seeking a correction from the mapmaker.

## What we left out of the summary

- Emergency committee chair Lee's "grounds for impeachment" remark and demand for records are what the original presents as social media remarks; we found no reports covering them. The remark itself is also conditional — if the government agreed to the deletion.
- Reports have not established whether the mapmaker's account (embassy consultations) or the foreign ministry's account (no consultation or agreement) is correct.
- Online community reactions quoted in the original were not reproduced.
