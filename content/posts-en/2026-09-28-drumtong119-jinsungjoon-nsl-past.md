A [Threads post by @sangwoosang84](https://www.threads.com/@sangwoosang84/post/Dd3UzB4GDTP) covering the same court-ruling content is noted here instead of in a separate post. Chair Jin's own radio remarks are covered in the [post on his "intent" remark](/post/2026-09-29-finf-jinsungjoon-mine-intent).

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It criticizes that even though the JCS announced it was very likely a North Korean mine, Defense Committee chair Jin Sung-joon (진성준) acknowledged North Korea's responsibility while saying it was hard to determine whether the intent was to harm troops.
- It cites a military court ruling quoted by Chosun Ilbo saying Jin organized a "Righteous Army Association" during his military service and argued for the withdrawal of U.S. forces from Korea, and that the ruling also records an argument that an armed revolution would need North Korean support. It states he received six years in prison at trial and three years on appeal for violating the National Security Act.
- It assesses that "his thinking from that time doesn't seem to have changed."

## What reports confirm

- A Chosun Ilbo column (Aug. 30) reported that Jin violated the National Security Act while serving as a short-term enlisted soldier after graduating college in 1989, and that a ruling by the 35th Division General Military Court states he viewed U.S. forces in Korea as an "occupying army" and argued for a "nationwide armed revolution" backed by North Korean support.
- According to the same column, Jin took the lead in founding a "Righteous Army Association" (의병회) within the unit and, at its founding meeting, reportedly said, "U.S. forces must withdraw to build a self-reliant national army." According to the column, the 35th Division General Military Court sentenced him to six years in prison, and the Army High Military Court to three years on appeal. The column criticizes him for being elected Defense Committee chair in June 2026 without reflection or explanation.
- Chair Jin reportedly said on the radio on Sept. 29 that "whether displaced or laid, North Korea is responsible," but that the response should differ depending on whether it was deliberately planted to injure troops.

## What we left out of the summary

- The assessment that "his thinking from that time doesn't seem to have changed" is the poster's own inference; we found no reported evidence showing Chair Jin's current thinking. We could not find, among the reports we checked, any statement from Jin's side about the past conviction.
