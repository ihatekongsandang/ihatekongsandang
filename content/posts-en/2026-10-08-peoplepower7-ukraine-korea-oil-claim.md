This post is based on the original caption and first image. The original image's "Zelensky: Korea is communist" and "Lee Jae-myung government helping communist Russia" are not found in reports, so we used a card made by this site instead of the original image. Related post (may be available only in Korean): [The issue of North Korean POWs in Ukraine being sent to South Korea](/post/2026-09-25-kimkyelee-nk-pow-ukraine).

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says Zelensky revealed that the Lee Jae-myung government is effectively helping Russia, a wartime ally of North Korea, and that according to U.S. and European intelligence the government supported shipments of war-related Russian fuel.
- The first image reads "Zelensky exposes the Lee Jae-myung government helping communist Russia" and "Zelensky: Korea is communist / Korea is helping Russia's war / The Lee Jae-myung government supported fuel shipments."

## What reports confirm

- Vladyslav Vlasiuk, sanctions adviser at Ukraine's presidential office, reportedly shared a Guardian report on Facebook on Oct. 6 (local time) saying South Korea supplied diesel and other products to Russia, writing that it "stands in stark contrast to South Korea's (Russia) sanctions policy" and claiming the imports from South Korea helped offset the impact of Russia's loss of refining capacity.
- Analyzing port records and ship-tracking data, the Guardian reportedly said seven tankers carried more than 176,000 tonnes of oil products (mostly diesel) from South Korean ports such as Ulsan and Yeosu to Russia's Far East, including Vladivostok, in 14 voyages in July–August.
- In a separate post, Vlasiuk reportedly rated as positive South Korea's expanded export controls on Russia and sanctions on ships and entities involved in North Korea–Russia military cooperation, and as negative its non-participation in the Russian oil price cap and continued purchases of Russian coal and LNG.
- South Korea's foreign ministry reportedly told the Guardian it "has expanded export controls under the Foreign Trade Act to include not only strategic items but also 1,402 non-strategic items that could be used for military purposes, and is strictly enforcing them." The criticism reportedly came amid continuing friction between Seoul and Kyiv over the disclosure of North Korean POWs being sent to South Korea.

## What we left out of the summary

- **"Zelensky reveals" and "Zelensky: Korea is communist"**: The reported criticism was a social media post by the presidential office's sanctions adviser, not President Zelensky, and we did not find any "Korea is communist" remark in the reports we checked.
- **"The Lee Jae-myung government supported fuel shipments" and "according to U.S. and European intelligence"**: The reported basis is the Guardian's analysis of port and ship-tracking data; we did not find reports saying the government supported the shipments or that U.S. or European intelligence had found so.
