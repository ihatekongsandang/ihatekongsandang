## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It explains that the chart's core claim is that "investigation through a special counsel and an internal prosecution inquiry could ultimately lead to the withdrawal of indictments in cases involving Lee Jae-myung."
- Route A: the National Assembly's "fabricated-indictment special counsel act" has a special counsel examine past investigations and indictments; the counsel's power to withdraw indictments, included in the original bill, is dropped at the president's request, but if the counsel sends its findings to prosecutors, they could use them to decide on withdrawal.
- Route B: a fact-finding team set up on the justice minister's order examines past investigations and indictments, and prosecutors could review its findings to decide whether to withdraw indictments.
- It explains that an indictment can be withdrawn until the first-instance verdict, after which the court dismisses the case — which differs from an acquittal. The captured Threads post says, "Don't you want the trials resumed?" and "Watch out for the Oct. 2 Criminal Procedure Act amendment."

## What reports confirm

- The fabricated-indictment special counsel bill was reportedly introduced in April, led by the Democratic Party, to investigate alleged fabricated investigations and indictments under the Yoon Suk Yeol (윤석열) government; a clause letting the counsel decide whether to maintain existing prosecutions reportedly sparked controversy over dropping President Lee's trials.
- At a Sept. 18 press conference, President Lee reportedly asked the National Assembly to "delete the provisions on transferring or disposing of existing indicted cases and focus only on finding the truth." The Democratic Party reportedly decided to remove the clause but on Sept. 29 held off on bringing the bill to a vote, saying it would revisit it after the parliamentary audit.
- A fact-finding team under the Justice Ministry's Committee on Respect for Human Rights and the Future of the Prosecution (launched in June) is reportedly examining 19 cases, including eight directly or indirectly involving President Lee, such as the Daejang-dong, Baekhyeon-dong and Wirye developments and the Ssangbangwool remittances to North Korea; in six of them, Lee is reportedly a defendant whose first-instance trial has been suspended.
- On his first day on Sept. 23, acting Prosecutor General Lee Jung-hyun (이정현) reportedly answered a question about possibly dropping the cases by saying, "The fact-finding team's inquiry is under way, so I think we have to look at its results." Legal circles reportedly offered interpretations and criticism that the findings could be used to justify withdrawing indictments.

## What we left out of the summary

- That both routes "ultimately reach withdrawal" is the chart author's projection. We found no reports that a withdrawal has been decided or ordered.
- The chart's "justice minister's order (April)" differs from the timing in reports, which say the fact-finding team was launched in June. We also could not confirm from reports whether the revised special counsel bill without the dismissal clause includes a procedure for "sending the counsel's findings to prosecutors."
- The original does not describe the "Oct. 2 Criminal Procedure Act amendment," so we did not summarize it.
