## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- Saying that a video of the Jeong Yul-seong (정율성) children's song contest held in Gwangju features Chinese-language songs and lyrics such as "Mr. Worker," it questions whether it is appropriate to hold a contest for children honoring the man who composed the Chinese People's Liberation Army anthem.
- It argues that children's education and culture must not be used for political or ideological purposes, and that the public should scrutinize the purpose and content of the event.

## What reports confirm

- It was reported in 2023 that since 2014, Gwangju MBC has held the "Jeong Yul-seong Children's Song Contest" for elementary school students nationwide, in which participants sing one Jeong Yul-seong song and one song of their choice, and that it received an annual subsidy of 50 million won from the city of Gwangju.
- Reports say that Jeong Yul-seong composed the Chinese People's Liberation Army anthem and the North Korean Korean People's Army anthem, that the contest's designated songs included one whose original lyric "The morning wind waves the Five-Star Red Flag" was changed to "Every morning we put on our school bags," and that at the 2018 contest one school sang half of this song in Chinese. The contest was also reportedly held together with a Confucius Institute established by the Chinese government.
- The then-ruling People Power Party criticized it as "mobilizing children to idolize a collaborator in communist aggression."

## 2026 discussion on restoring the bust (update)

- The @cheongram_magazine reel says Gwangju has continued commemorative projects for Jeong Yul-seong (정율성) on the grounds of Jeong's anti-Japanese activities and Korea–China cultural exchange, and argues that, given that Jeong composed the PLA anthem and the Korean People's Army march, whether to honor Jeong with a children's song festival and a bust needs public debate and review (the poster's opinion).
- The bust on Jeong Yul-seong Street in Yangnim-dong, Nam District, Gwangju, was unveiled in July 2009 after Nam District received it through a civic group from a Chinese local government, and was later damaged by a member of a conservative group, it was reported. In April 2026, the Chinese Embassy and the Consulate General in Gwangju reportedly asked Gwangju and others to restore the bust, and Nam District said it would consider restoration and resuming commemorative projects after gathering residents' views once the appeals ruling for the person who damaged it is issued.
- On June 9, 2026 (local time), a delegation including then–Gwangju Mayor Kang Gi-jung (강기정) and Nam District head Kim Byung-nae (김병내) met Jeong's daughter in Beijing, and Gwangju was reported to be finalizing a schedule to reinstall the bust and arranging a visit by the family. In response, 38 groups including a civic coalition against the Jeong Yul-seong park reportedly held a press conference on June 16 demanding a halt to reviving the projects and restoring the bust.
- We could not confirm from reports whether the bust has since been reinstalled.
- A [Threads video by @houchin.official](https://www.threads.com/@houchin.official/post/DeeOji5CYWh) (Oct. 14, "A PLA anthem button in front of a Gwangju apartment") shows, as of Oct. 6, buttons labeled "March 1 March," "We Are Happy" and "Chinese People's Liberation Army anthem" installed on a glass panel at the Jeong Yul-seong street exhibit on Jeongyulseong-ro in Yangnim-dong (12 video frames reviewed). These buttons are not new: a 2018 report already described the Jeong Yul-seong street exhibit, running 233 meters along the wall of the Yangnim Humansia apartment complex, as having the score of the PLA anthem and a button that plays the song when pressed. "How can this be in South Korea?" is the poster's opinion.
