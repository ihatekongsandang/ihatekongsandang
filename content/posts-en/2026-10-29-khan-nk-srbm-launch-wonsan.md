This post is based on news reports. A related post on North Korea's submarine-launched cruise missile test (may be available only in Korean): [North Korea's SLCM announcement and the JCS](/post/2026-10-14-2pro-nk-slcm-not-announced).

## What reports confirm

- The Joint Chiefs of Staff said North Korea fired some 10 projectiles, including short-range ballistic missiles, into the East Sea from the Wonsan area at around 12:30 p.m. on Oct. 29, according to reports. Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that the missiles flew about 250 km and were known to be 600 mm multiple rocket launchers. The military reportedly judged that North Korea had trained using various firepower assets, including ballistic missiles.
- The JCS reportedly said "South Korea and the U.S. closely monitored the launch from the preparation stage" and "detected and tracked the missiles immediately after launch."
- The launch came six days after the Ministry of National Defense, in an Oct. 23 statement in the name of defense policy chief Kim Hong-cheol (김홍철), again proposed inter-Korean military talks to prevent accidental clashes along the Military Demarcation Line in the DMZ, according to reports.

## What we left out of the summary

- The interpretations that the launch was a de facto rejection of the talks proposal and an extension of high-intensity provocations to raise tension near the front line are "interpretations from inside and outside the military" reported in the article and were left out of the factual summary. We could not confirm from this article whether North Korea announced the launch.
