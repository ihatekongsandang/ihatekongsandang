This post is based on the original caption; the video content was not reviewed. The caption was cut off partway through, so its ending could not be checked.

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It introduces how, on Sept. 28, 2014, Hong Kong citizens occupied the Central and Admiralty districts demanding fully direct elections for the chief executive, and how umbrellas raised against police tear gas became the symbol of the democracy movement. It notes the occupation was dispersed after 79 days.
- It explains that the 2019 protests against amending the extradition law grew into a large-scale democracy movement, and that the scene of police entering a subway car at Prince Edward Station on Aug. 31 to subdue passengers and protesters together caused great shock.
- It says that after the National Security Law took effect in 2020, arrests and prosecutions of pro-democracy figures and the dissolution of parties and groups followed, and its title describes this as overlapping with "Korea 40 years ago."

## What reports confirm

- Joshua Wong is described as the figure who led the 79-day Umbrella Movement demanding direct election of the chief executive in 2014, and was reportedly arrested by Hong Kong police on Aug. 30, 2019, ahead of a planned large-scale protest.
- In November 2024, a Hong Kong court reportedly handed down prison terms to 45 people in the "Hong Kong 47" case, which treated the 2020 pro-democracy primary as a conspiracy to subvert state power; Joshua Wong was reportedly sentenced to four years and eight months, and former University of Hong Kong professor Benny Tai to 10 years.
- While serving his sentence, Joshua Wong reportedly additionally admitted in September 2026 to a National Security Law charge of "collusion with foreign forces."

## What we left out of the summary

- Rumors of a death that circulated at the time regarding the Prince Edward Station incident were denied by Hong Kong authorities and remain unconfirmed, so they were not covered here.
