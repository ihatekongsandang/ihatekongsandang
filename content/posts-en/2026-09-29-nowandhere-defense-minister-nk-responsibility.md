## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says Defense Minister Kang Sin-cheol (강신철) acknowledged an armistice violation and North Korea's responsibility and even mentioned countermeasures, so the head of the Defense Ministry has in the end admitted that North Korea did it.
- It says the president told an official event the same day "don't make fake news" about the North Korean mine, and sarcastically wonders whether the president will call the minister's remarks fake news too.

## What reports confirm

- Minister Kang reportedly told the National Assembly's National Defense Committee on September 29 that "the armistice violation is clear and responsibility lies with North Korea, so we will take corresponding measures" and that "we will implement the necessary response measures step by step." Asked by lawmaker Chun Ha-ram (천하람) whether it could be confirmed that the mine had been laid rather than washed away, he reportedly answered that "it does not appear to be a washed-away mine."
- Kang reportedly said North Korea appears to have laid the mines while fortifying its border line, and one more North Korean resin anti-personnel mine was reportedly found near the accident site. He reportedly did not disclose the specific response measures and said the National Security Council (NSC) was not convened over the incident.
- President Lee reportedly said at a Cabinet meeting the same day that "we will transparently uncover the truth and take the necessary measures according to it," adding, "I hope there will be no endangering of national security by stoking groundless conspiracy theories."

## What we left out of the summary

- The original quotes the president as saying "don't make fake news," but the reported wording was "groundless conspiracy theories," and the president also spoke of taking measures based on the facts, so it is hard to see his remarks as directly contradicting the minister's.
