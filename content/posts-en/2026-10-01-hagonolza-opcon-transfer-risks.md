## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It introduces President Lee Jae-myung's remark that "self-reliant defense is sovereignty, and regaining wartime operational control (OPCON) will put a period on it," and says it will point out the risks this implies.
- It explains that wartime OPCON is the authority to direct which units go where and what operations they carry out in war, and that the transfer (officially called "transition") changes the commander of the combined Korea-U.S. forces in wartime from a U.S. general to a Korean general while keeping a system of fighting together with the United States.
- It lists four concerns: (1) if Seoul and Washington disagree, joint operations could be delayed (a problem that already exists); (2) insufficient command capability could break the link between defense and counterattack, so capability must be verified; (3) even if a Korean general commands the combined forces, the U.S. retains command of its own forces, so U.S. assets cannot be mobilized at will; and (4) the final decision to use U.S. nuclear weapons rests with the U.S. president.
- It concludes that the biggest risk is rushing the transfer before being ready.

## What reports confirm

- At the 78th Armed Forces Day ceremony at Gyeryongdae in South Chungcheong Province on Oct. 1, President Lee reportedly said "self-reliant defense is sovereignty" and said, in effect, that regaining wartime OPCON would put a period on the task of self-reliant defense. At a luncheon after the ceremony, he reportedly said "no country in the world depends on others or another country for its military command" and that he would "swiftly complete the return of wartime operational control to regain our status as a truly self-reliant, independent nation and a proper democratic republic."
- At the November 2025 Security Consultative Meeting, Seoul and Washington reportedly agreed to complete, by November 2026, the second of three verification stages — Full Operational Capability (FOC) — for the Future Combined Forces Command, a condition for the transfer; only after the final stage, Full Mission Capability (FMC), would the two defense ministers report a specific transfer date to their leaders.
- U.S. Forces Korea Commander Xavier Brunson reportedly told the U.S. House in April that the target for the transfer is the first quarter of 2029, and that once conditions are met, approval by both presidents is required. Seoul reportedly wants a swift transfer, while U.S. military authorities have reportedly applied relatively conservative standards in judging whether conditions are met.

## What we left out of the summary

- The command structure the original describes (the commander's nationality changing, the U.S. retaining command of its own forces, and authority over nuclear use) was not checked point by point against the reports we reviewed. The four concerns and the conclusion that "a rushed transfer is the biggest risk" are the poster's analysis.
- The original's phrase "the risks this implies" is the poster's assessment; no report says the president's remark carried any particular intent.
