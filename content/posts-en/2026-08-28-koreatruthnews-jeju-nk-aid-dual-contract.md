This post is based on the original caption and thumbnail; the video content was not reviewed. The original relays an exclusive TV Chosun report of Aug. 28, 2026. Because the original thumbnail's "disguised aid to North Korea" is a definitive claim, we used a card made by this site.

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- Citing TV Chosun, it says a humanitarian aid project to North Korea, reportedly arranged when Jeju Governor Oh Young-hun (오영훈) met "North Korean operative against the South" Ri Ho-nam (리호남) in Beijing in February, was carried out with two contracts so that North Korean authorities were not told the goods came from South Korea and were made to think China sent them.
- It cites criticism that inter-Korean cooperation funds built from taxpayers' money were used without transparency toward either North Korea or the South Korean public.

## What reports confirm

- Jeju Province said it reached an overall inter-Korean cooperation agreement with North Korean officials in Beijing in February 2026, and two North Korean officials including Ri Ho-nam, a former counselor at the North Korean Embassy in China, and then-Governor Oh reportedly attended. Jeju said it later received the Unification Ministry's approval and shipped about 160 million won worth of goods, including dialysis machines, hallabong tangerine saplings and pine wilt control chemicals, to Nampo in North Korea via Dalian, China, according to reports.
- TV Chosun reported that the contracts Jeju and an aid group submitted to the government were of two kinds with identical content but different parties: one listing four parties — North Korea's Korean Association for Supporting the Disabled company, China's Dandong Jingxie trading company, Jeju Special Self-Governing Province and the South-North Business Federation — and another listing only the North Korean and Chinese companies, with the North Korean side submitting the two-party version to North Korean authorities. The government received both and approved contact with North Koreans and the shipment, it reported.
- On Sept. 1, a Unification Ministry official reportedly said "three-party agreements are institutionally recognized under the enforcement decree of the Inter-Korean Exchange and Cooperation Act" and "rather than a double contract, there are two agreements, between A and B and between B and C, under an A-B-C three-party agreement," adding that the ministry approved, under the relevant law, a project Jeju pursued with its own funds. People Power Party lawmaker Na Kyung-won (나경원) had earlier called in the budget committee for the approval to be revoked, according to reports.
- TV Chosun quoted government officials as saying it is hard to see a legal problem but the explanation of why two contracts were used is lacking, and reported that Jeju said it "cannot disclose" documents confirming the goods' arrival in North Korea.
- Former Governor Oh left office after the June 2026 local elections; the current Jeju governor is Wi Seong-gon (위성곤).

## What we left out of the summary

- "Disguised as Chinese support" is TV Chosun's characterization; the Unification Ministry describes it as a legally recognized three-party agreement, so assessments differ. No violation of law has been established.
- The original caption describes the funding as "inter-Korean cooperation funds built from taxpayers' money," while the Unification Ministry said it was "a project Jeju pursued with its own funds," so this post does not draw a conclusion about the nature of the funding.
