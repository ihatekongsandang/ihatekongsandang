This post is based on the original caption; only the first image was reviewed. The original is a post from Rep. Lee Jin-sook (이진숙)'s own account.

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says the president's family is violating the Farmland Act while refusing to move his late father's grave, and that conducting a nationwide farmland survey on farmers in the name of the principle that farmland belongs to those who farm it, while leaving his own family as an exception, is a double standard.

## What reports confirm

- It was reported in 2023 that the grave of President Lee Jae-myung's parents in Myeongho-myeon, Bonghwa County, North Gyeongsang Province, was built on a field (farmland) that cannot be used as a cemetery — his father's grave in 1986, with his mother buried in the same grave in 2020 — and that suspicions were raised of violating the Farmland Act and the Funeral Services Act because no farmland-conversion permit or cemetery-installation report procedures were completed.
- A Bonghwa County official at the time reportedly said, "Farmland with a grave on it is hard to legalize under the relevant laws; the only option is to relocate it," and "Following the procedures, we plan to issue a restoration order to the owner and then impose a fine."
- On Sept. 28, 2026, Rep. Lee Jin-sook reportedly claimed that Bonghwa County had issued a restoration order dated Dec. 18, 2025 (compliance deadline Dec. 20, 2026), and that the landowner, the president's older brother, had received orders three times — in 2023, 2024 and 2025 — but only paid fines without moving the grave.

## What we left out of the summary

The three restoration orders and the fine payments were reported as Rep. Lee Jin-sook's claims; the reports we found this time contain no confirmation or explanation from Bonghwa County or the president's side. The line that the family would "even break the law because it is the grave site that made him president" is the poster's interpretation.
