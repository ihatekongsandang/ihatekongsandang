## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says former Rep. Chris Stewart, who served on the House Intelligence Committee until 2023, wrote in the Washington Examiner that China is more likely to target U.S. allies than the United States directly, pointing to South Korea.
- It says the House and Senate Armed Services Committees, for the first time, directed the Pentagon to brief Congress on Chinese Communist Party influence in South Korea and risks to U.S. forces (House report deadline Dec. 1; Senate report May 1, 2027), and that on Sept. 28 Rep. John Moolenaar, chair of the House Select Committee on the CCP, wrote Secretary of State Marco Rubio that South Korea's recent economic and diplomatic shift toward Beijing poses a serious risk to the United States.

## What reports confirm

- In a Sept. 28 letter to Secretary of State Rubio, House Select Committee on the CCP Chairman John Moolenaar reportedly wrote that "recent shifts in South Korea's economic, technological and diplomatic posture toward China pose significant risks for the United States," citing expanded leader-level exchanges between South Korea and China, South Korean companies' semiconductor investment in China and discriminatory treatment of American firms, and asked the State Department for a briefing on Korea–China relations and the administration's response by Oct. 31.
- The South Korean Embassy in Washington reportedly told VOA on Oct. 5 that "the South Korea–U.S. alliance is the foundation of our foreign policy and a linchpin for peace and prosperity on the Korean Peninsula and in the Indo-Pacific."
- The Senate Armed Services Committee's report accompanying the fiscal 2027 National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) for the first time included an item on "assessing the malign influence of the Chinese Communist Party in South Korea," directing the defense secretary to brief by May 1, 2027, and the House Armed Services Committee's bill report also sought a related briefing by Dec. 1, according to reports (the House provision was sponsored by Rep. Ronny Jackson).

## What we left out of the summary

- We could not find former Rep. Chris Stewart's Washington Examiner op-ed, so it was not included in the summary.
- The NDAA report requests are directions from congressional committees, not an official U.S. government conclusion about South Korea.
