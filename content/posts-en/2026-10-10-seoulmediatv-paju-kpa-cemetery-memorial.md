This post is based on the original reel's caption ("Live: invading the Paju KPA cemetery memorial") and thumbnail ("Patriots who came to invade the Paju KPA memorial," with a photo of a participant holding a "6.25 was a southward invasion" sign). The original thumbnail shows the face of a private participant, so we used a card made by this site instead. A [Threads post by @wonkkongtuwang](https://www.threads.com/@wonkkongtuwang/post/DeB3sO_H-LA) and an [Instagram post by @rekor.rgt](https://www.instagram.com/p/Dd_guP3gYh7/) about this memorial's poster before the event were put on hold because no reports covered it, but reports have now appeared, so they are noted here (see "What we left out of the summary").

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It posts footage from the scene, calling the participants who went to protest the Paju KPA cemetery memorial "patriots," and promises a follow-up video.

## What reports confirm

- On Oct. 10, the "21st Anti-Imperialist Independent Reunification Prayer Ceremony — KPA Cemetery Memorial" was held at the North Korean soldiers' cemetery in Dapgok-ri, Jeokseong-myeon, Paju, Gyeonggi Province, it was reported. The poster bore the slogan "Remove U.S. Forces Korea!!!!" and listed the "U.S. Troop Withdrawal Struggle Headquarters" as host and "Minjungseon Peace Church" as organizer, and a banner at the site reportedly read "Drive out USFK and hasten independent reunification."
- The memorial was announced for 2 p.m. but held in the morning instead, and Pastor Lee Jeok (이적) reportedly wrote on Facebook, "We heard that reactionary forces would disrupt the memorial, so we moved it from the afternoon to the morning and finished safely without clashing with them." Photos posted by the pastor reportedly showed about 10 people in purple vests.
- In the afternoon the same day, conservative civic groups such as the "Citizens' Group to Protect the History of 6.25," North Korean defectors, youth groups and Paju residents held a press conference and protest in front of the cemetery, with 180–200 participants by the organizers' count, and police set up a police line and deployed riot units, it was reported. Defector Lee Eun-taek (이은택), head of Paradise TV, reportedly said they would raise the cemetery issue with Paju City, the Ministry of Patriots and Veterans Affairs and the defense ministry.
- The cemetery was created by the defense ministry in 1996, in line with the Geneva Conventions, to gather remains of North Korean and Chinese soldiers killed in the Korean War and others; the Chinese remains were later repatriated, according to reports. A memorial hosted by other groups was also held here in March 2019, sparking controversy over politicians' attendance (Newsis).
