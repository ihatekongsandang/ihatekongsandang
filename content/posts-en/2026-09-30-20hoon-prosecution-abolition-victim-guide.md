## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It explains that the Prosecutors' Office will be abolished on Oct. 2, 2026, with indictments handled by the Public Prosecution Agency, investigations of serious crimes by the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency and other investigations by the police, and that prosecutors will no longer be able to investigate.
- It says prosecutors may interview victims to decide whether to indict, but the statements and materials obtained that way cannot be used as evidence of guilt, so victims may have to repeat their statements to an agency with investigative authority.
- It lists three things for victims and complainants to check — (1) which agency can investigate their case, (2) submitting evidence only to an agency with lawful investigative authority, and (3) whether further statements or procedures are needed after submission — and calls the abolition "a system that has become harder" for victims and complainants.

## What the law confirms

- Article 245-13 of the Criminal Procedure Act, amended on Sept. 29, 2026 and taking effect on Oct. 2, provides that prosecutors may confirm facts by hearing opinions from suspects, persons involved and others when necessary to decide whether to indict (paragraph 1); that complainants and others may request an interview with a prosecutor to state their views (paragraph 2); and that **prosecutors may not use in court, as evidence, statements or materials obtained through such fact confirmation or interviews with complainants** (paragraph 3).
- The same amendment deleted Article 196, the former provision on investigations by prosecutors, and Article 4 of the Public Prosecution Agency Act, taking effect on Oct. 2, defines prosecutors' duties as deciding on and maintaining indictments, requesting warrants, and consulting with and supporting judicial police officers on criminal investigations, among others.

## What we left out of the summary

- The drug case the poster says they uncovered as a prosecutor, and the view that "that speed can no longer be expected," are the poster's own experience and opinion.
- This post did not confirm from the law the detailed criteria for which agency investigates which types of cases (such as the "seven major crimes"). For an actual case, check with the investigating agency or a lawyer.
