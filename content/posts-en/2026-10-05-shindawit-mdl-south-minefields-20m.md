## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- The first image reads "North Korea invaded our land on the eastern front and installed a barbed-wire fence," and the second captures the headline of an Oct. 5 Yonhap News article, "Several suspected North Korean minefields south of the MDL, crossing about 20 m on average."
- The caption and third image say the JCS identified several presumed North Korean minefields south of the MDL that intrude about 20 m on average; that North Korea has put up to about 7,000 people a day into fortification work this year; that North Korean MDL crossings rose from 9 in 2024 to about 50 this year, about 40 of them repeated at one spot because GP troops use it as a patrol route; and that the military is consulting the UN Command on MDL mismatches while the North has not responded.

## What reports confirm

- At a defense ministry briefing on Oct. 5, the JCS reportedly made a final determination that the Sept. 21 DMZ mine blast on the western front was caused by "a resin anti-personnel mine that North Korea installed south of the MDL under the pretext of its so-called border fortification work," and said it removed the North Korean minefield south of the MDL at the site that day (JCS Vice Chairman Kwon Dae-won (권대원)).
- JCS intelligence analysis chief Kim Sung-yoon (김성윤) reportedly said, "Several areas south of the MDL where North Korean troops are presumed to have laid mines have been identified, and they intrude about 20 m south of the MDL on average," with the width of intrusion varying by area. The blast site was reportedly confirmed by GPS to be about 10 m south of the MDL.
- JCS operations chief Yang Jin-hyeok (양진혁) reportedly said North Korea has put up to about 7,000 people a day into work this year, focused on minefields and fences; that of the 250 km MDL, clearing land is nearly done while fences, minefields and tactical roads are about 40–60% complete; and that the work is expected to finish within two years.
- North Korean MDL crossings were reportedly identified 9 times in 2024, 17 times in 2025 and about 50 times this year, and the JCS reportedly explained that about 40 were repeated at one spot because North Korean GP troops patrol a point about 10 m south of the MDL daily, using cleared work areas as a patrol route.
- South Korea's military reportedly uses the MDL on military maps established by the U.S. National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency in 2004, while the UN Command uses a separate MDL, so the lines do not match in some areas; the military is reportedly consulting the UN Command on aligning them. The defense ministry reportedly proposed military talks on the MDL to North Korea last November, with no response.

## What we left out of the summary

- **"North Korea invaded our land on the eastern front and installed a barbed-wire fence"**: According to reports, there is one area where a North Korean fence is **suspected** of crossing the MDL, but further checks are needed, and the JCS reportedly said the area is not in the Army 21st Division's sector (Yonhap's subheading: military says "needs checking"). Whether it is on the eastern front, and whether the fence actually stands south of the MDL, was not confirmed in reports.
- Reports do not give the specific locations of the "several" presumed minefields south of the MDL. The JCS reportedly said it would also investigate and remove North Korean minefields south of the MDL in other areas.
