This post is based on the original caption; the video content was not reviewed. We also did not confirm from the video who the "former Naval Academy superintendent in the video" mentioned in the caption is.

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- Change is needed in academy education, but expanding joint education and revamping curricula are possible under the current system, so those should be implemented first and their results verified.
- Merging organizations and building a new campus without verification would take a long time and an enormous budget, and if it fails, the sunk costs and transitional burdens would fall on the cadets.
- A policy should be judged by "what has improved compared with before."

## What reports confirm

- In July 2026, the Ministry of National Defense reportedly announced a basic plan to create a four-year "Korea Armed Forces Academy" at Jaundae in Daejeon by merging the Army, Navy and Air Force academies.
- At a Defense Ministry work report on Aug. 5, President Lee Jae-myung reportedly said the Korea Military Academy had been at the center of past military coups and that creating a merged armed forces academy could reduce the possibility of the military intervening in politics.
- In an Aug. 6 statement, 46 former Army, Navy and Air Force chiefs of staff reportedly called for a review, saying, "Presenting the merger of the three service academies as a preventive measure is a mismatch between diagnosis and prescription."
- Former Naval Academy superintendent Lee Ki-sik (이기식) reportedly said in a discussion with The Financial News that "the responsible order is to start reform now but decide on a merger after verification is complete," and claimed that building the Jaundae campus would take 10 years and that "there is even talk of more than 4.2 trillion won."
- In the same discussion, Chung-Ang University professor Choi Young-jin (최영진) reportedly took the opposing view that a merger, not partial fixes, is needed, saying, "The problems written in a 1986 report are still there in the current report."

## What we left out of the summary

The cost of "more than 4.2 trillion won" is an estimate that former superintendent Lee Ki-sik relayed as "there is even talk of"; we could not confirm it as a project cost officially announced by the Defense Ministry.
