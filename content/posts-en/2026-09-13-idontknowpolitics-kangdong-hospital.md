Another post covers the same matter along with a different poster's criticism: [Thread criticizing medical equipment aid to North Korea](/post/2026-09-21-dotori-nk-medical-equipment). An [Instagram reel by @geonwoo.vibes](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Ddo6-eIzKY8/) (Sept. 23) criticizing the same aid is noted here instead of in a separate post. It says aid of about 9.3 billion won — 1,431 items of 166 types of medical equipment — is being pursued, and argues that the safety of the public and troops should come before spending tax money on North Korea, which threatens South Korea's security with nuclear weapons and missiles (the poster's opinion). The figure of 'about 9.3 billion won' does not appear in the reports this post checked.

## What reports confirm

- The government has reportedly drawn up a plan to provide 166 types of essential medical equipment for diagnosis, surgery and treatment to a single hospital, Kangdong County Hospital in Pyongyang, which was completed in November 2025 as the first achievement of North Korean leader Kim Jong Un (김정은)'s "Regional Development 20×10 Policy."
- Unification Ministry spokesperson Yoon Min-ho (윤민호) reportedly said, "We received a UN Security Council sanctions exemption last May," and "We are preparing so that the related project can start right away if the North responds." Because UN Security Council Resolution 2397 bans the transfer of industrial machinery to North Korea, a sanctions exemption is reportedly required for medical equipment aid.
- The funding will reportedly come from the "other economic cooperation projects" budget of the Inter-Korean Cooperation Fund, and the ministry reportedly reported the plan to the National Assembly's Foreign Affairs and Unification Committee in August.
- The aid items were later reportedly set at 1,431.

## Additional reports on Oct. 7 (update)

- According to materials the Ministry of Unification submitted on Oct. 7 to People Power Party Rep. Kim Dae-sik (김대식), the government reportedly requested a sanctions exemption from the UN Security Council's North Korea sanctions committee to send 1,431 items of 166 types of medical equipment (about $6.75 million, or 9.3 billion won) to Kangdong County Hospital through the Korea Foundation for International Healthcare, and after being notified of approval on May 22, set Nov. 22 — within six months of approval — as the tentative shipping deadline. It reportedly also reported a plan to ship via Incheon, Dandong in China and Nampo, with Hyundai Asan tentatively chosen as the shipper, and to send a six-member joint monitoring team to check against diversion.
- A [Threads post by @xhfnfn2](https://www.threads.com/@xhfnfn2/post/DeL_BlGk861) describing this as "couldn't wait and sent 9.3 billion won to North Korea" is noted here instead of in a separate post. The Gangwon Ilbo (강원일보) headline it captured originally read "…sends medical equipment" but now reads "…announces plan to provide medical equipment," and the article describes a shipping plan. We found no report that the equipment has actually been sent.

## Kim Yo-jong's statement on Oct. 8 (update)

- In an Oct. 8 statement carried by the Korean Central News Agency, Kim Yo-jong (김여정), director of the Workers' Party General Affairs Department, reportedly said the unification ministry had "spoken recklessly" in citing North Korea's worsening health situation and help for vulnerable groups, calling it "an insult to our state, a mutated political provocation and a grave violation of sovereignty that goes against our national law," and said "medical equipment provided by South Korea cannot enter our territory, nor is there any way to push it in." Kim was reported to have called the plan "the second part of the farce" following the "mine blast incident," and to have claimed that Minister Chung Dong-young (정동영) had "confessed" that it is hard to conclude the blast was a deliberate North Korean attack. Kim reportedly said that if the South wants to restore relations it "should first show maximum respect, consideration and sincerity," while reaffirming that the North has "nothing whatsoever to exchange or be entangled with" South Korea.
- An [Instagram post by @brief_tag](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DeOGZ3oTCli/) summarizing the statement is noted here instead of in a separate post. Its thumbnail text — "Kim Yo-jong: give the medical equipment to the dogs / Your charity is too dirty to accept / And Chung Dong-young takes our side well" — is not found in the statement; it is the poster's rewording.
- An [Instagram post by @wikipost_daily](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DeODF2pynH5/) criticizing the aid the same day ("Lee Jae-myung: let's help North Korea / 9.3 billion won to a North Korean nuclear-development area / Injured soldier gets 80 million won") is also noted here. Reports confirm that People Power Party lawmaker Park Choong-kwon (박충권) claimed some equipment could be diverted to military use because the Second Economic Committee, which handles North Korea's munitions industry, is in Kangdong County, and that the unification ministry's position is that treating residents is separate from the nuclear issue. We did not find reports describing Kangdong as a "nuclear-development area." The "80 million won" refers to a veterans-affairs expert's reported analysis that the battalion commander who lost an ankle in the mine blast could receive a lump-sum disability payment of about 89.25 million won if recognized as a "combat injury" (pension and national-merit compensation are separate); it is not an amount the government has finalized and paid.

## What we left out of the summary

No aid has actually been provided yet, and in Kim Yo-jong's Oct. 8 statement North Korea reportedly said it would not accept it. We could not directly confirm the original's expression "Ready To Go" in the reports we found this time.
