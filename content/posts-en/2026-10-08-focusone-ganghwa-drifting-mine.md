The thumbnail's "Civilians will soon be stepping on mines too / Is talking with North Korea still the priority?" is the poster's opinion, so we used a card made by this site instead of the original image. Unlike the mines laid south of the Military Demarcation Line in the western-front DMZ, this mine is **believed to have drifted by water**. An [Instagram reel by @real_news_rn](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DeVwIIAPItv/) on a North Korean army cap found on a beach in Goseong, Gangwon Province (Oct. 11, "Goseong is trembling too / After mines, a North Korean army cap found / What is South Korea hiding?") is also noted here instead of in a separate post (see the "Goseong, Oct. 10" section). ## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- The caption relays reports that on Oct. 8 an anti-personnel mine believed to have been lost from North Korea was found again, after 54 days, near Daebinchang Beach on Jumun Island, Seodo-myeon, Ganghwa County, and that the Marines detonated it.
- The thumbnail reads "'Mine found' on a Ganghwa beach / Civilians will soon be stepping on mines too / Is talking with North Korea still the priority?"

## What reports confirm

- At about 3:20 p.m. on Oct. 8, a worker tidying the area near Daebinchang Beach on Jumun Island found and reported an anti-personnel mine, and the Marine 2nd Division's explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) team collected and detonated it on site, with no casualties or property damage, it was reported.
- The Marines reportedly said "according to the EOD team's own examination, it is presumed to be an anti-personnel mine lost from North Korea," and it is believed to have washed down from the North during summer downpours.
- A mine was also found in Ganghwa's island areas on Aug. 15, and 17 mines including wooden-box mines and anti-personnel mines were found one after another in Ganghwa in July–August, according to reports. The military asked people not to touch suspicious objects and to report them to military units or police.

## North Korean army cap on Jajakdo Beach, Goseong, Oct. 10 (update)

- At 6:30 a.m. on Oct. 10, a barefoot-walking group doing regular marine litter collection at the end of Jajakdo Beach in Jugwang-myeon, Goseong County, Gangwon Province, collected a cap presumed to be a North Korean army cap; its rank insignia had already come off, and it is believed to have drifted down long ago, it was reported. Debris from the North reportedly washes up periodically on Goseong's coast, a divided border area.
- The @real_news_rn caption itself notes there are no signs that North Korean soldiers came down or infiltrated and that the cap is presumed to have been lost long ago. The thumbnail's "Goseong is trembling too" and "What is South Korea hiding?" are the poster's words; we found no basis in reports for anything being hidden.

## What we left out of the summary

- "Civilians will soon be stepping on mines too" and "Is talking with North Korea still the priority?" are the poster's opinions. No damage was reported from this discovery.
- As the original caption also notes, how the mine was lost and how it arrived have not been confirmed.
