This post is based on the original caption and the first frame of the reel. We did not review what is said in the video. Other posts on the constitutional amendment (some may be available only in Korean): [Key issues in the constitutional amendment](/post/2026-09-20-hagonolza-constitution-amendment-points) · [The 2018 announcement to drop "free," reversed](/post/2026-07-04-idontknowpolitics-2018-freedom-deletion).

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- The caption claims that if a constitutional amendment passes: (1) President Lee Jae-myung gets a second term, for 8 years in total; (2) "free" is deleted from "free democracy" (자유민주주의) in the Constitution (a change of system to "social communism"); (3) electronic and mail voting; (4) the sweeping introduction of a CBDC (central bank digital currency); (5) a parliamentary cabinet system; and (6) large-scale government ownership of real estate.
- The first frame reads "2027 constitutional amendment crisis! How will it end?!"

## What reports and the law confirm

- **A second term and 8 years**: Article 128(2) of the Constitution states that "amendments to the Constitution for the extension of the term of office of the President or for a change allowing for the reelection of the President shall not be effective for the President in office at the time of the proposal for such amendments." The National Assembly Research Service reportedly answered a written question from People Power Party Rep. Kim Sang-hoon (김상훈) in August 2026 that "under any theory, it does not apply to the president at the time of the amendment," and reportedly also took a negative view, in effect saying it would be hard to overturn established scholarly consensus, of deleting that clause first during the president's term and then amending for consecutive terms.
- **The amendment actually proposed**: Six parties other than the People Power Party reportedly proposed an amendment in April 2026 that would add the spirit of the Buma Democratic Protests and the May 18 Democratic Uprising to the constitutional preamble and tighten the requirements for martial law; the May 7 plenary vote reportedly failed for lack of a quorum because the People Power Party did not take part.

## What we left out of the summary

- We found no report that any amendment proposal contains, or that anyone is pursuing, the deletion of "free," electronic and mail voting, a CBDC, a parliamentary cabinet system or government ownership of real estate. These items are also absent from the reported contents of the April 2026 amendment described above.
- "A change of system to social communism" is an unfounded assertion and was left out of the summary.
- The 2018 episode in which an announcement to drop "free" during the Democratic Party's amendment talks was reversed four hours later is covered in the related post above.
