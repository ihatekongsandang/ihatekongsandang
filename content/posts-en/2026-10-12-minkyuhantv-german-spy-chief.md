## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- Citing the arrest of a former German intelligence chief on espionage charges, it argues South Korea is "in no position to laugh at another country," saying that Park Jie-won (박지원), convicted in the illegal North Korea remittance case, served as NIS director and that Unification Minister Chung Dong-young (정동영) publicly mentioned the location of a North Korean nuclear facility, and asks, "Should we catch spies first, or vet intelligence chiefs first?" The thumbnail reads "The spy-catching intelligence chief was a Russian spy."

## What reports confirm

- Germany's federal prosecutors said on Oct. 6 they had arrested August Hanning, who headed the Federal Intelligence Service (BND) from 1998 to 2005, and Hanning's former chief of staff (aide) on espionage charges, it was reported. Prosecutors reportedly said the aide passed about 2,000 documents, many containing classified information, to Hanning from 2010 until at least 2022, and that Hanning used them in a private consulting business, including writing analysis reports for foreign intelligence officials.
- Prosecutors did not specify which country received the information, and German public broadcasters WDR and NDR and the Süddeutsche Zeitung reportedly said the case is not linked to Russia.
- Former NIS Director Park Jie-won was indicted by the 2003 special counsel on the North Korea remittance case; the charge of taking 15 billion won in bribes from Hyundai was ruled not guilty, while leading the remittance and related acts were found guilty, according to reports. Minister Chung's mention of a uranium enrichment facility in Kusong and the subsequent controversy over U.S. limits on intelligence sharing are covered in the [post on the NIS's exclusion from the working group](/post/2026-10-08-shindawit-us-nis-cyber-group-excluded).

## What we left out of the summary

- **"Was a Russian spy"**: According to reports, the Hanning case is not related to Russia, and Hanning was head of Germany's Federal Intelligence Service, not South Korea's NIS.
- "Vet intelligence chiefs first" and "a national disgrace" are the poster's opinions.
