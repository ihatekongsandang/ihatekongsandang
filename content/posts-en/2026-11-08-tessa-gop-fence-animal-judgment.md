This post is based on the original text and its single attached image. The image is a card made by another outlet, so we did not reproduce it. We read the time Threads shows (11:40 p.m., Nov. 7) as UTC and dated the post Nov. 8 Korea time.

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- The text is a single line, "Do animals carry cutters these days…", and the attached card shows a photo of a front-line fence with the words "Front-line fence damaged, but the military judged it 'the work of an animal' — controversy."

## What reports confirm

- The incident: a woman in her 30s entered the area north of the Civilian Control Line in Cheorwon, Gangwon Province, without permission on Oct. 24, kept moving north, and on Oct. 25 tried to damage the sensor mesh attached to a front-line general outpost (GOP) fence before the military took her into custody that day, reports said. The Joint Chiefs of Staff said it was investigating with related agencies and that "as far as we know, there are no signs of espionage," according to reports.
- The military's response: the JCS said at an Oct. 27 briefing that the Ground Operations Command had been inspecting the unit's overall guard operations since Oct. 26, reports said. The JCS reportedly said that the Civilian Control Line is run on the premise of public cooperation, so saying the line "was breached" confuses it with GOP guard operations. Reports also said criticism arose because the military could not take the woman into custody for about 20 hours after first spotting her.
- The "animal" judgment: at a Nov. 6 press conference, Sung Il-jong, a People Power Party member of the National Assembly's defense committee, reportedly claimed that the military first assumed the woman was a civilian who had come to gather wild greens, saw no signs of espionage and ended the search, and that when a sensor-cut alarm went off at 10:55 a.m. on Oct. 25, it judged the cut to be "by an animal" because no person was visible on surveillance video. Sung reportedly said the military identified a person on video around noon, resumed the search and took her into custody, and called for disciplining commanders, starting with the corps commander.

## What we left out of the summary

- The "cut by an animal" judgment was reported as Sung's account. We found no report of the military confirming or rebutting that point.
- Reports differ on whether the sensor mesh was actually cut: some say she tried and failed, while others say the alarm went off and she cut it with pliers and scissors.
- The woman is a private individual, so we did not include her identity or what was reported about her manner during questioning.
