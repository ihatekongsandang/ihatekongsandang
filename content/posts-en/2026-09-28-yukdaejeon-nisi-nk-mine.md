This post is based on the original text and the one image attached to the original post. The original cites NewsVerse as its source.

## What reports confirm

- On Sept. 28, NewsVerse reportedly quoted multiple government sources as saying "the first analysis at the National Forensic Service is understood to be finished" and "the first analysis reached a tentative conclusion that the blast is presumed to be from a North Korean mine." It also reported that the NFS identified explosive residue on evidence such as the soldiers' protective suits and mine boots.
- After the accident, the military reportedly asked the NFS for a detailed analysis of the evidence to determine whether the blast came from a North Korean or South Korean mine.
- At a regular briefing the same day, the JCS reportedly said it "has not been officially notified of the analysis results by the NFS."
- Testimony that troops at the site saw an unexploded mine has also reportedly continued.

## What we left out of the summary

The analysis results were at the stage of a report citing anonymous government sources; there was no official announcement from the NFS or the military.

## Later developments

On the evening of Sept. 28, 2026, the JCS announced interim findings saying "it is not our mine and very likely a North Korean mine" (without yet concluding whether it was recently laid or displaced). Details: [JCS interim findings](/post/2026-09-28-pagememo-jcs-interim-result).
