## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It summarizes that former President Yoon Suk Yeol (윤석열) cited "the threat of North Korean communist forces" as the rationale for martial law on Dec. 3, 2024, and that after martial law was lifted, the Constitutional Court removed him from office in April 2025.
- It says that under the Lee Jae-myung government that followed, there came a softening of "main enemy" language, the halt and removal of loudspeakers aimed at North Korea, the phased restoration of the Sept. 19 military agreement, the dismantling of the Defense Counterintelligence Command, the guardhouse baton controversy and a sanctions exemption for medical equipment aid to North Korea, and it presents both sides: critics see excessive appeasement of the North and weakened deterrence, while defenders see prevention of accidental clashes and military reform.
- However, it writes that because these policies were pursued after martial law, it cannot be concluded that Yoon foresaw them or that they retroactively prove martial law was justified. The thumbnail text ("martial law was right"), by contrast, is the poster's own wording.

## What reports confirm

- At his July 2025 confirmation hearing, Unification Minister Chung Dong-young (정동영) reportedly answered "I do not agree" when asked whether North Korea is the main enemy, and Labor Minister nominee Kim Young-hoon (김영훈), who was appointed labor minister later that month, reportedly said he also agreed with this, then after a day-long back-and-forth answered, "Yes, it is the main enemy."
- In his 2025 Liberation Day address, President Lee Jae-myung said, "The South and the North are not enemies" and "We will not pursue any form of unification by absorption and have no intention of engaging in any hostile acts," and pledged the preemptive, phased restoration of the Sept. 19 military agreement.
- Reports say the Ministry of National Defense began removing fixed and mobile loudspeakers aimed at North Korea in August 2025, and in June 2026 dismantled the Defense Counterintelligence Command after 49 years, dividing its counterintelligence, security and investigation functions among new organizations.
- It was reported that in late 2025 and early 2026 it became known that some frontline units had considered or implemented, under JCS guidelines, having guardhouse sentries carry telescopic batons instead of firearms, sparking controversy, and that the Army belatedly withdrew the plan.
- Reports say the Ministry of Unification obtained a UN Security Council sanctions exemption in May 2026 for medical equipment aid worth about 10 billion won, is pursuing a plan to send 1,431 items of 166 types of equipment to Kangdong County Hospital in Pyongyang, and is in a position to carry it out if North Korea accepts.

## Later developments (updated Nov. 9)

- On Aug. 3, 2026, the Ministry of National Defense set up three organizations to take over the command's functions: the Defense Counterintelligence Headquarters handles counterintelligence and defense-industry intelligence, the Security Investigation Unit of the Defense Criminal Investigation Service investigates espionage, leaks of military secrets and acts benefiting the enemy, and the Defense Security Support Group handles security audits and security incident inquiries within the military, while the surveillance of personnel trends and personnel intelligence functions were abolished, according to reports. The same article said the government cited the command's dispatch of troops to the National Assembly and the National Election Commission and its involvement in arrest teams during the Dec. 3 martial law as reasons for dismantling it (Pennmike).
- The same article reported concerns among security experts that counterintelligence capability has declined as intelligence and investigation are split across agencies, and said the ministry's position is to share intelligence and respond jointly through a "security investigation council" of the three organizations.
- The office of People Power Party lawmaker Kang Sun-young (강선영) said records from the active-duty years of about 590,000 reservists are kept at the Defense Counterintelligence Headquarters and should be destroyed upon discharge; the ministry said "the headquarters can never use the retained records" and that it is holding them temporarily because procedures to transfer them to the ministry have not been completed, Seoul Economic Daily reported on Oct. 21.

## What we left out of the summary

- We did not address any judgment on the legitimacy of the Dec. 3 martial law. The original caption also states that the legitimacy of martial law cannot be concluded from the policy changes.
