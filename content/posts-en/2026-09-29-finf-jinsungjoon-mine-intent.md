## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says that the very day after the military concluded the mine had been deliberately laid by North Korean forces, the ruling party's Defense Committee chair caused controversy by suggesting that intent "cannot yet be determined."
- It notes voices pointing to a gap in perception, as the ruling party's committee chair argued for caution on intent at a time when the JCS had called it a provocation.

## What reports confirm

- At an emergency briefing on the night of Sept. 28, the JCS reportedly said "it has been established that the blast was not caused by our mine" and "the possibility that North Korean forces laid the mine is very high," warning that all responsibility lies with North Korea; the first forensic analysis reportedly found TNT and RDX but no tetryl, the explosive used in the South Korean M14 mine. The JCS's official wording was "very high possibility," so the original's "concluded it was deliberately laid" goes a step further.
- On KBS radio on Sept. 29, Jin reportedly said, "Whether it was washed away or laid, North Korea is basically responsible," but added, "There is a big difference in intent between laying a mine deliberately to injure our soldiers and a mine laid elsewhere being washed down and causing an accident," and said the response should be decided comprehensively with that in mind.
- Citing the possibility that the mine was part of fortification near the MDL under the "two hostile states" line, he reportedly said stern protest measures are needed, but that the 2015 box-mine attack — in which North Korea crossed the MDL and planted mines at a South Korean gate — was different, and that immediately comparing the two to justify a military response called for caution.
- On allegations of a delayed investigation, he said the military's explanation was reasonable and called independent lawmaker Han Dong-hoon's (한동훈) claims "excessive and groundless." Han reportedly shot back, "Then did they plant it for fun?"
