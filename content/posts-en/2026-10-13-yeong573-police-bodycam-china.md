## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says People Power Party lawmaker Park Sang-woong (박상웅), at the National Police Agency audit on Oct. 12, raised the allegation that 14,000 police body cams were Chinese products passed off as Taiwanese and pressed the agency on weak origin checks and the risk of personal data leaks.
- It adds that the National Police Agency said no evidence of data leakage has been confirmed so far and that it will act on the results of an audit.

## What reports confirm

- In May 2025, the National Police Agency announced a body cam project worth 19.5 billion won, stating that Chinese-made devices would be excluded from bidding, and selected a consortium of company A and KT, which said its product was made in Taiwan on an OEM basis, receiving about 14,000 body cams, according to reports. Before the selection, another bidder raised the Chinese-origin suspicion and the Public Procurement Service informed the police, but the police went ahead with the selection and did not carry out a planned inspection of the Taiwan factory, it was reported.
- At the police's request, the Korea Testing Laboratory under the trade ministry disassembled and compared company A's product with a body cam from Chinese radio maker H, and reportedly concluded that their exteriors, internal components and assembly were mostly the same, stating that "company A's product is highly likely to be Chinese company H's product with only some exterior elements changed." Gyeonggi Nambu Provincial Police are investigating company A on charges including fraud under the Act on Aggravated Punishment of Specific Economic Crimes, while company A reportedly denies the charges, saying the product is a Taiwanese OEM product.
- At the audit, acting National Police Agency Commissioner Kim Jong-cheol (김종철) reportedly said "I apologize for not closely examining the ordering and delivery process," and answered that detailed examinations by the Korea Internet & Security Agency and others found no particular network security problems and no backdoor. Saying that recalling the devices without clear evidence of leaks would create gaps in policing, Kim reportedly said follow-up measures would depend on the Board of Audit and Inspection's findings and that damages claims would be considered if the devices prove to be Chinese-made.

## What we left out of the summary

- Whether the body cams are Chinese-made is still under investigation and audit and has not been established. According to the police, no signs of data leakage have been found so far. The video's "it could even threaten national security" is Rep. Park's concern.
