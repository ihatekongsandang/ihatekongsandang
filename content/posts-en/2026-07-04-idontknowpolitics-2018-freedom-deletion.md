This post is based on the original caption; the video content was not reviewed. The original was posted on July 4, 2026, but the event it covers took place on **Feb. 1, 2018**.

## What reports confirm

- On Feb. 1, 2018, the Democratic Party reportedly held a caucus meeting on constitutional amendment and went through the process of adopting a party position.
- In a briefing right after the caucus, then-floor spokesperson Je Yoon-kyung (제윤경) reportedly said, "We decided to revise the 'free democratic' basic order in Article 4 of the Constitution to the broader 'democratic' basic order."
- Article 4 of the current Constitution states, "The Republic of Korea shall seek unification and shall formulate and carry out a policy of peaceful unification based on the free democratic basic order."
- When controversy arose, the Democratic Party reportedly issued a statement around 10:30 p.m. the same day, about four hours after the briefing, saying, "The content that 'free' would be dropped was wrongly conveyed because of the spokesperson's error, as there were many provisions," and that the word "free" would be kept as is.
- Then-Liberty Korea Party spokesperson Jeong Tae-ok (정태옥) reportedly claimed in a Feb. 2 commentary that "it was not the spokesperson's mistake … it is clear they canceled it in the face of fierce backlash."
- Reports at the time also said there was debate over other elements of the amendment, such as the social economy and the public concept of land.

## What we left out of the summary

"A simple announcement error vs. a case showing the direction of discussions at the time," at the end of the caption, is a question the original poster put to readers. Whether it was an error or reflected actual discussions is disputed between the party's explanation and its opponents' claims, and this post does not judge either way.
