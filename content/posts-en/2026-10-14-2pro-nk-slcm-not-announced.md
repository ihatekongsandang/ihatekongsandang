This post is based on the original text and its thumbnail. Because the original thumbnail's "Lee government and defense ministry covered up the missile launch" is a definitive claim not supported by evidence, we used a card made by this site. Related post (may be available only in Korean): [Military declines to submit Chinese and Russian KADIZ entry counts to the National Assembly](/post/2026-10-13-brieftag-kadiz-data-withheld).

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It claims that North Korea launched a strategic cruise missile from a submarine on the 14th but that the defense ministry and the Lee Jae-myung government were found to have covered it up.
- Calling it a provocation three days after a ballistic missile launch, it says suspicions are arising that the government hid the information out of concern over falling approval ratings.

## What reports confirm

- North Korea's KCNA said on Oct. 15 that "the Missile Administration test-fired two types of submarine-launched strategic cruise missiles in the East Sea on the 14th," according to reports.
- Reports said that while North Korean missile launches are usually disclosed by the JCS on the day, there was no announcement from the military this time. News1 explained that the South Korean military announces in real time only North Korean ballistic missile launches and does not classify cruise missiles as ballistic missiles.

## What we left out of the summary

- "Found to have covered it up" and "hid the information over concern about approval ratings" are the poster's claims, and we found no reports supporting them. Reports cited the military's practice of not announcing cruise missiles in real time as the reason there was no announcement. We could not confirm for this post any JCS explanation of whether the military detected this launch in advance.
