This post is based on the original caption and its three images (a Threads post capture, an explanation card and an illustrated card). We do not reproduce the swearing in the caption and capture, and because the third image portrays Gyeonggi Province giving 155.8 billion won to North Korean children, we used a card made by this site instead of the original images.

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says Gyeonggi Province, citing financial difficulties, left 2.7 billion won in childcare funding — daycare operating costs and better pay for childcare staff — out of its supplementary budget, and that infant meal support and a monthly 230,000 won payment to daycare teachers will also end. By contrast, it says 14.1 billion won was budgeted for childcare support for foreigners' children and the government budgeted 155.8 billion won for North Korean infants, criticizing that "they squeeze taxes from citizens and hand out lavishly." Its hashtags include "impeach Choo Mi-ae."

## What reports confirm

- After 2.7 billion won needed to keep existing programs — support for the standard infant childcare curriculum, operating costs for daycare centers for ages 0–2, and pay improvements for childcare and Nuri-class teachers — at the same level through year-end was left out of Gyeonggi's supplementary budget, the Gyeonggi Daycare Center Association held rallies in front of the provincial office on Oct. 6–8 (about 2,000 on the first day by the association's count; 1,800–2,000 by police) and said it would push for closing all centers if its demands are not met, it was reported.
- Gyeonggi Governor Choo Mi-ae (추미애) wrote on Facebook on Oct. 10, "I make it clear that the misunderstanding that childcare funding was arbitrarily cut is not true," attributing it to the main budget (drawn up by the previous provincial administration) failing to secure enough funds, and said that, considering national support of 1 million won per facility per month, the province "unavoidably adjusted" its operating support per child from 12,600 won to 6,000 won, according to reports. The provincial council was set to handle its own amended budget on Oct. 12, and any increase reportedly requires the governor's consent.

## What we left out of the summary

- **"155.8 billion won for North Korean infants"**: The 155.8 billion won for North Korean infants and others in 2026 was reported as a unification ministry (inter-Korean cooperation fund) budget item, not Gyeonggi's budget. It is a budgeted amount, not actual spending.
- **"14.1 billion won for foreigners' children"**: We did not find a 14.1 billion won Gyeonggi budget for childcare support for foreigners' children in the reports we checked.
- "Impeach Choo Mi-ae" and similar are the poster's opinions.
