## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says the military spotted by satellite last September terrain changes near the MDL believed to be traces of North Korean mine-laying, and that the accident site was about 10 m from that area and about 10 m south of the MDL. Because the area was a surveillance blind spot, it says, the accident happened during an operation on Sept. 21 to clear a patrol route to check it.
- It says reporters asked why the military was only now talking about a blind spot when it had known a year earlier, and that the JCS replied it had "continuously consulted the UN Command" and "intended to make a final judgment after checking the site together."
- It criticizes that the terrain change had never been disclosed to the press and that concrete follow-up measures were missing.

## What reports confirm

- At a Sept. 29 briefing, the JCS reportedly said it first spotted by satellite, in the second half (September) of last year, terrain changes believed to be traces of North Korean mine-laying; that it had been consulting the UN Command to verify them; and that the blast occurred on Sept. 21 while clearing a patrol route to that point.
- Asked when it requested a site investigation from the UN Command and which side delayed the consultations, it reportedly said it did "not know exactly" and would check further. The military said it is reviewing additional surveillance assets to eliminate blind spots.
- Before the accident, however, at a regular Defense Ministry briefing on Sept. 14, the JCS spokesperson reportedly responded to a report that North Korea had crossed the MDL and created six minefields by saying "we have identified some terrain changes and are consulting closely with the UN Command on the possibility of mine-laying." The existence of terrain changes was thus mentioned before the accident; what is new is the disclosure of when they were first spotted (last September).

## What we left out of the summary

- A quote from an anonymous military source ("they kept it quiet all this time") was not included because its source could not be verified.
