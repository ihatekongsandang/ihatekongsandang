## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It criticizes that the same camp that treated May 18 as sacrosanct and blocked even academic debate on it now invokes "cinematic imagination" in the film "Assassin(s)" to hint that former President Park Chung-hee (박정희) was behind the killing of his wife, Yuk Young-soo (육영수).
- It argues this is a malicious smear that even exploits the grief of losing a spouse, aimed at portraying the leader who led industrialization as a monstrous figure.

## What reports confirm

- At the Aug. 15 Liberation Day ceremony in 1974, Mun Se-gwang (문세광), a Korean resident of Japan, attempted to shoot President Park Chung-hee but instead fatally shot Yuk Young-soo; the special investigation headquarters at the time announced the act was carried out on the orders of North Korea and Chongryon (the pro-Pyongyang organization of Koreans in Japan).
- The film "Assassin(s)" reportedly does not name the culprit explicitly, but its plural title and plot reportedly raise the possibility of an actor other than Mun — a plot by the Park Chung-hee government or the Korean Central Intelligence Agency — sparking a controversy over historical distortion; a complaint was reportedly filed with the Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency against the production company's CEO, the director and the writers for defamation by false statement.
- Rep. Lee Jin-sook reportedly raised the question, "Why does 'cinematic imagination' apply only to former presidents Park Chung-hee and Chun Doo-hwan (전두환)?" The filmmakers reportedly stated their position that the work "reconstructs records from that time with added cinematic imagination."

## What we left out of the summary

- We did not address exactly which scenes and how the film hints at this, since the film itself was not reviewed. The phrase "May 18 is sacrosanct, so don't even debate it" is Rep. Lee's own characterization; her earlier controversy over a May 18 debate is covered in the related post above.
