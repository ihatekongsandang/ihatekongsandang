This post is based on the original caption. The original's five images list 16 items under the heading "Let's not forget Lee Jae-myung's achievements," but they mix private-life allegations and unfounded assertions (such as "7 people around (Lee) died" and "fake hunger strike"), so we did not reproduce them and used a card made by this site. An [Instagram reel by @monarchreport25](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DeM5hiTFZBi/) covering the same issue in English (Oct. 8, "Stand Trial: Lee's frozen cases and the American who can't go home") is also noted here instead of in a separate post (see "What we left out of the summary").

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says President Lee Jae-myung faces five criminal trials as a defendant — Daejang-dong, Wirye, Baekhyeon-dong and Seongnam FC; the Gyeonggi corporate card; the Ssangbangwool remittance to North Korea; the Public Official Election Act; and perjury solicitation — all suspended since taking office under Article 84 of the Constitution. It notes the charges have not been finalized as guilty and that legal responsibility must be judged in court, arguing "if it was political persecution, prove innocence; if there is something to answer for, be judged at trial."

## What reports confirm

- Article 84 of the Constitution provides that "the president shall not be charged with a criminal offense during the tenure of office except for insurrection or treason," and legal opinion has reportedly been divided over whether "charged" covers continuing trials already under way.
- In June 2025, the Seoul High Court left the date open in the election-law case (retrial on remand), and the Seoul Central District Court did so for the Daejang-dong, Wirye, Baekhyeon-dong and Seongnam FC cases, citing Article 84; in July, the Ssangbangwool remittance trial was also postponed, leaving all five of the president's trials suspended, it was reported. The courts reportedly explained that this was so the defendant could devote themselves to constitutional duties as president and to ensure continuity of state affairs.

## What we left out of the summary

- The 16 items in the original images mix charges under indictment, matters already ruled on for other people, private-life allegations and unfounded assertions, so they were not included in the summary.
- The @monarchreport25 reel covers the Oct. 3 rally (about 28,000 on Sejong-daero by unofficial police estimate), the suspended trials, the abolition of the Prosecutors' Office on Oct. 2, and the case of Morse Tan, a former U.S. ambassador-at-large indicted for defaming President Lee and barred from leaving Korea, and says "Five trials, zero verdicts." However, the election-law case had first- and second-instance rulings and a Supreme Court remand, and the perjury-solicitation case had a first-instance ruling, so "zero verdicts" is not accurate. We could not confirm in this post that members of the U.S. Congress called for respecting Tan's free-speech rights.
