## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says President Lee Jae-myung faces five criminal trials as a defendant — Daejang-dong, Wirye, Baekhyeon-dong and Seongnam FC; the Gyeonggi corporate card; the Ssangbangwool remittance to North Korea; the Public Official Election Act; and perjury solicitation — all suspended since taking office under Article 84 of the Constitution. It notes the charges have not been finalized as guilty and that legal responsibility must be judged in court, arguing "if it was political persecution, prove innocence; if there is something to answer for, be judged at trial."

## What reports confirm

- Article 84 of the Constitution provides that "the president shall not be charged with a criminal offense during the tenure of office except for insurrection or treason," and legal opinion has reportedly been divided over whether "charged" covers continuing trials already under way.
- In June 2025, the Seoul High Court left the date open in the election-law case (retrial on remand), and the Seoul Central District Court did so for the Daejang-dong, Wirye, Baekhyeon-dong and Seongnam FC cases, citing Article 84; in July, the Ssangbangwool remittance trial was also postponed, leaving all five of the president's trials suspended, it was reported. The courts reportedly explained that this was so the defendant could devote themselves to constitutional duties as president and to ensure continuity of state affairs.

## Update on the North Korea remittance case (Oct. 12)

- The @ansanheart reel reposts another channel's edit of Fox News footage with Korean subtitles, including a scene in which a guest says Lee called U.S. Forces Korea an "occupying army" and that Lee is also accused of sending money to the North Korean government while Gyeonggi governor. The caption says foreign media reports do not themselves prove guilt and that allegations should be judged by evidence and due process (the poster's opinion).
- As the caption says, former Gyeonggi Vice Governor for Peace Lee Hwa-young (이화영), indicted in the Ssangbangwool remittance case, had a sentence of 7 years and 8 months upheld by the Supreme Court and recently said a retrial request was being considered, it was reported. President Lee was also indicted in the same case, but as noted above, that trial is suspended.
- Police also reportedly dropped (dismissed without referral) a complaint against President Lee alleging benefiting the enemy and abuse of power in July 2026, saying "there is no evidence to establish that the $8 million was provided to North Korea on the suspect's instructions." That was a decision on a separate complaint and is a different process from President Lee's remittance trial pending in court.
- We could not confirm the date of the Fox News broadcast or the guest's identity. Lee's "occupying army" remark was reportedly made on July 1, 2021, the day Lee, then Gyeonggi governor, declared a presidential run, about U.S. forces right after liberation ("the country could not start cleanly, having collaborated with the U.S. occupying army"); when it drew controversy, Lee replied that "it is correct that they were an occupying army." However, we could not check against the original broadcast how the video characterized it.

## What we left out of the summary

- The 16 items in the original images mix charges under indictment, matters already ruled on for other people, private-life allegations and unfounded assertions, so they were not included in the summary.
- The @monarchreport25 reel covers the Oct. 3 rally (about 28,000 on Sejong-daero by unofficial police estimate), the suspended trials, the abolition of the Prosecutors' Office on Oct. 2, and the case of Morse Tan, a former U.S. ambassador-at-large indicted for defaming President Lee and barred from leaving Korea, and says "Five trials, zero verdicts." However, the election-law case had first- and second-instance rulings and a Supreme Court remand, and the perjury-solicitation case had a first-instance ruling, so "zero verdicts" is not accurate.
