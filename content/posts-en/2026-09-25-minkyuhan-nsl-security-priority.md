## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- At a time when troops were injured in a DMZ blast under an armistice, it argues that the commander-in-chief raising the National Security Act's "praise and incitement" clauses was a mismatch in message priorities, and that the president should first clearly explain his views on North Korea and his security principles.

## What reports confirm

- On Sept. 21, three officers were reportedly injured in a blast presumed to be a mine during a DMZ search operation.
- President Lee Jae-myung reportedly referred to the 2004 National Security Act debate at a community meeting with Korean residents in Mexico on Sept. 24 (local time), saying, "Abolishing it entirely all at once was too much, so the ruling and opposition parties had nearly agreed to delete at least a few toxic clauses," and that "recently, police have been conducting search-and-seizure raids on people for studying North Korea's Juche ideology."

## What we left out of the summary

The image's "abolish the National Security Act entirely" is the opposite of the reported remarks. The president referred to the failed agreement to delete toxic clauses, not to a full abolition.
