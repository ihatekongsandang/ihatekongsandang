## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says less than two days remain before the Prosecutors' Office is abolished and replaced by the new Public Prosecution Agency (공소청), and that 6.5 billion won has been spent so far erasing prosecution markings and putting up Public Prosecution Agency signs.

## What reports confirm

- Ahead of the Prosecutors' Office's abolition on Oct. 2, the Ministry of Justice reportedly earmarked 6.5 billion won in reserve funds for costs related to the transition to the Public Prosecution Agency, including removing prosecution logos and lettering from the exterior walls and windows of 67 prosecutors' office buildings nationwide.
- Beyond building signage, business cards, ID badges and promotional materials, as well as subway route maps and road signs, also need to be changed, so costs are reportedly expected to rise further; Seoul Metro reportedly estimated that renaming subway station signs and replacing announcements would cost about 1.24 billion won.

## What we left out of the summary

- The original states the 6.5 billion won has "been spent," but reports describe it as reserve funds that were "earmarked," and the actual amount disbursed has not been confirmed. The original's spelling of the agency's name (공수청) appears to be a typo for the Public Prosecution Agency (공소청).
