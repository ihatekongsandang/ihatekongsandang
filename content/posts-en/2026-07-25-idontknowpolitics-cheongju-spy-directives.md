This post is based on the original caption and thumbnail posted on July 25, 2026; the video content was not reviewed. The original cites TV Chosun and court rulings as its sources. A [reel on the indictment](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DakB-mQTJ-B/) posted by the same account on July 9 covered the same TV Chosun report (64 communications, a directive to foment division among conservatives before the 21st general election, 20,000 dollars in operating funds, and past activity in candidate Moon Jae-in's special adviser group), so it is noted here instead of in a separate post. That reel said "the first-instance trial is under way," but the defendants' sentences had already been finalized in 2025 (see the reports below). Another post on the same case: [Revisiting the North Korean directive on a "first-stage goal of prosecution reform"](/post/2026-08-03-pagememo-nk-prosecution-reform-directive).

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It relays that prosecutors said members of the "Cheongju spy ring (Chungbuk Comrades Association)," investigated by the NIS and police in 2021, contacted North Korean agents via secure USB drives and encrypted email and reported on domestic political and election trends.
- It says the directives included responding to public opinion concerning former President Park Geun-hye (박근혜), trends in the conservative camp, analysis of the presidential election landscape and reports on the movements of then-Prosecutor General Yoon Suk Yeol (윤석열). The TV Chosun screen in the thumbnail shows captions such as "64 communications with the North" and "anti-conservative struggle before the general election."
- It writes that some members had been active in candidate Moon Jae-in (문재인)'s special adviser group during the 2017 presidential election, and that the ruling camp at the time drew a line, saying they were "only some among many special advisers and have no connection."
- It poses the question of whether, in security cases, the facts and the courts' judgments matter more than political background.

## What reports confirm

- It was reported in August 2021 that North Korea directed the Cheongju spy ring side to "create a mood for dismantling the NIS," and also issued a directive to the effect of highlighting the then-Liberty Korea Party as "a party that discriminates against women."
- Reports say the defendants were indicted on charges of forming the organization on the directives of North Korean agents in 2017 and receiving 20,000 dollars in operating funds, and that some had been active in candidate Moon Jae-in's campaign labor special adviser group during the 2017 presidential election.
- In March 2025, the Supreme Court reportedly finalized a two-year prison sentence for the chairman and five-year sentences each for the vice chairman and adviser, and found the charge of organizing a criminal group not guilty. The liaison's sentence was reportedly reduced from 14 years at first instance to five years on appeal and finalized in September 2025.

## What we left out of the summary

- Reports do not confirm any basis for regarding the campaign or government of the time as connected to the case merely because of past activity in the special adviser group, so we stated only the fact of that activity.
