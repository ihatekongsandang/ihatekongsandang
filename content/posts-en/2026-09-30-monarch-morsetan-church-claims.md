This post is based on the original (English) caption, and we used a card made by this site instead of the original image. The claims are remarks by Morse Tan, former U.S. Ambassador-at-Large for Global Criminal Justice, as relayed by the poster; Tan has reportedly been indicted and is on trial for spreading falsehoods about (defaming) President Lee Jae-myung. Related post (may be available only in Korean): [President Lee's five criminal trials suspended](/post/2026-10-09-gotdemcatch-lee-five-trials-suspended).

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says Morse Tan is sending a warning from Seoul: six American missionaries who moved Bibles and basic goods toward North Korea were arrested and jailed under the current government; there have been unprecedented raids on churches, prosecutions of pastors and threats to close Christian schools; the day after Tan was commissioned as a missionary in Korea, officials floated a tax of up to 50% on missionary income; and a church-dissolution bill under discussion would let the state dismantle a congregation and seize its property.

## What reports confirm

- Six Americans in their 20s to 50s were reportedly arrested for trying to float about 1,300 plastic bottles containing rice, $1 bills and Bibles toward the North from Mangwol Fort in Ganghwa County, Incheon, early on June 27, 2025 (violating the Framework Act on the Management of Disasters and Safety), and in August were booked without detention and referred to prosecutors. They reportedly said they "tried to send Bibles to North Korea for missionary purposes," and police said no backing organization was identified. Ganghwa County had been designated a danger zone since November 2024, with an administrative order banning the release of leaflets and the like toward the North.
- A Civil Act amendment introduced in January 2026 by independent lawmaker Choi Hyuk-jin (최혁진) would allow revoking the incorporation of nonprofit corporations, including religious ones, that violate the separation of religion and state by intervening in political activity, and would vest their remaining property in the state treasury, prompting criticism from churches that it is a "church dissolution law," according to reports.

## What we left out of the summary

- **"Six American missionaries jailed"**: According to reports, they were booked without detention and referred to prosecutors; we found no report that they were jailed. The incident also occurred shortly after the current government took office (June 4, 2025).
- **"A tax of up to 50% on missionary income"**: We did not find this in the reports we checked.
- **"Unprecedented church raids, prosecutions of pastors, threats to close Christian schools"**: The original does not specify which cases it means, so this post did not check them.
- The Civil Act amendment on dissolving religious corporations is a lawmaker's bill; we found no report that it has passed the National Assembly.
