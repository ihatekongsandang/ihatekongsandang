This post is based on the original caption; the video content was not reviewed. The caption covers only up to the first-instance ruling (15 years in prison), so the subsequent appellate and Supreme Court results are noted as well.

## What reports confirm

- Former Korean Confederation of Trade Unions (KCTU) organizing and labor-dispute bureau chief Mr. Seok reportedly received, around Nov. 15, 2022, about two weeks after the Itaewon disaster, a directive email from an agent of North Korea's Cultural Exchange Bureau saying, "Taking this extraordinary disaster as an opportunity, we hope you will actively carry out organizing work to unleash, to the fullest, the anger of all walks of life, focusing on creating within society a situation like the 2014 truth-seeking struggle over the 'Sewol' ferry disaster."
- Reports say Seok and others were arrested and indicted in May 2023 on charges including receiving North Korean directives 102 times between 2018 and 2022 and meeting North Korean agents abroad, and that the first-instance court (November 2024) sentenced Seok to 15 years in prison.
- The appellate court (May 15, 2025) reportedly found some charges not guilty — for example, not recognizing the existence of the secret organization that was the premise of the charges — and sentenced Seok to nine years and six months, while the other officials indicted with him reportedly received three years in prison or were acquitted.
- The Supreme Court reportedly finalized the appellate ruling on Sept. 25, 2025.

## What we left out of the summary

The "15 years in prison" in the original is the first-instance sentence; the finalized sentence is nine years and six months. We could not specifically confirm from the reports we found this time what activities were actually carried out after the Itaewon-related directive was received.
