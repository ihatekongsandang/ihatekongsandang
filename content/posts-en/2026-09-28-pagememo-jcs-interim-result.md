## What reports confirm

- On Sept. 28, 2026, the Joint Chiefs of Staff (JCS) reportedly announced interim findings on the Sept. 21 blast in the DMZ sector of the Army's 25th Division, saying the blast point was about 10 m south of the Military Demarcation Line (MDL) and that it was "very likely a North Korean mine."
- The National Forensic Service reportedly found TNT and brownish residue on three items, including a ballistic helmet and a ballistic vest, but no metal fragments; two craters believed to be from two separate explosions were reportedly found at the site.
- JCS operations chief Yang Jin-hyeok (양진혁) reportedly explained that South Korean mines whose main charge is TNT produce many metal fragments and have a lethal radius of about 20 m or more, and, citing the absence of metal fragments and the small lethal radius, said "it is not our mine and very likely a North Korean mine." He also said there were no records of South Korean mines laid in the area.
- One combat engineer reportedly said he saw a square object 5–10 cm underground about 1 m from the first blast point, which the JCS reportedly assessed as a "brown resin anti-personnel mine similar in shape to a North Korean box mine."
- The JCS reportedly said that if the mine is finally determined to have been laid by North Korean forces, "all responsibility lies with North Korea" and it "will take appropriate and corresponding measures."

## What we left out of the summary

The JCS has not yet concluded whether North Korean troops recently laid the mine at that spot directly or whether a mine from elsewhere was displaced; it plans to announce final conclusions after the site investigation and an assessment with the United Nations Command.
