This post is based on the original caption (the full text of the statement).

## What the original post claims (summary of the statement)

- The statement says that abolishing on Oct. 2 the Prosecutors' Office, which has existed for 80 years, will create a structure in which the police monopolize investigative authority, making it difficult to respond to violent, organized, drug and international crimes and to provide relief to vulnerable victims.
- In creating the Public Prosecution Agency, the title of its top official remains "prosecutor general" because of Article 89 of the Constitution, resulting in a patchwork system.
- It argues that the abolition of the Prosecutors' Office, a law to punish judges and prosecutors, a four-tier trial system and an increase in the number of Supreme Court justices amount to the president "erasing his crimes" and destroy the judicial system.

## What reports confirm

- At 11 a.m. on Sept. 21, 2026, former candidate Kim Moon-soo (김문수) reportedly held a press conference with the "Lawyers Against the Destruction of the Judiciary" outside the Supreme Prosecutors' Office in Seocho-gu, Seoul, and stated his opposition to abolishing the Prosecutors' Office.
- On Oct. 2, the Prosecutors' Office will reportedly be abolished and the Public Prosecution Agency (공소청), in charge of indictments and maintaining prosecutions, and the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency (중대범죄수사청), which investigates seven categories of serious crimes, will be launched, with prosecutors' investigative authority abolished. Concerns were also reportedly raised at the press conference that rulings dismissing prosecutions on the grounds that investigations went beyond their permitted scope could increase.

## What we left out of the summary

"The president erasing his crimes" and "there are many people with criminal records in the Democratic Party" are political claims in the statement, not facts confirmed by reports. The positions of the government and ruling party were not included in the reports we found this time.
