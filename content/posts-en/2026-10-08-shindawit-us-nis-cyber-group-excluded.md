## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- The first image reads "The U.S. refused NIS participation in a meeting on North Korean hacking," and the second captures the headline and subheadings of the Oct. 8 Dong-A Ilbo exclusive.
- The caption and third image say the U.S. asked to exclude the NIS from the U.S.–South Korea–Japan working group on North Korean cyber threats; that the NIS reportedly missed the fifth meeting in late June for the first time; that the U.S. apparently raised concerns about the NIS's security of U.S. classified information; and that limits on sharing information about North Korean nuclear facilities have stayed in place for over six months. It adds that the foreign ministry only announced that crypto-asset theft and other issues were discussed, and that some in the government say "the NIS is a burden on South Korea–U.S. relations."

## What reports confirm

- Citing current and former senior government sources, Dong-A Ilbo reported on Oct. 8 that the Trump administration asked Seoul to exclude the NIS from the fifth meeting of the trilateral working group on North Korean cyber threats, held in Washington in late June, and that it was reportedly the first time the NIS did not attend. The working group was launched under the 2023 Camp David summit agreement, the report said.
- The same report said the U.S. is reportedly raising concerns about the NIS's security of U.S. classified information, and that U.S. limits on sharing satellite intelligence on North Korean nuclear facilities have reportedly remained in place for more than six months since Unification Minister Chung Dong-young (정동영)'s remark about the "Kusong nuclear facility." After the fifth meeting, the foreign ministry's press release only said the countries shared assessments of North Korea's crypto-asset theft and laundering and IT worker activity and discussed responses, according to the report.
- Dong-A Ilbo also reported that some in the government say "the NIS is becoming a burden on South Korea–U.S. relations," and that Foreign Minister Cho Hyun (조현) reportedly argued with NIS Director Lee Jong-seok (이종석) at a standing committee meeting of the National Security Council, citing U.S. concerns about the NIS.
- In response, the NIS reportedly said on Oct. 8, "As the article says, there was some misunderstanding months ago, but it is already in the past," adding that "there is currently no misunderstanding between the South Korean and U.S. intelligence authorities" and that it "feels responsible that movements of an intelligence agency that should not be made public were revealed in the media" (Newsis).

## Follow-up reports on Oct. 8–9 (update)

- Asked in writing about South Korea–U.S. intelligence cooperation, a senior U.S. administration official reportedly said on Oct. 9 (local time) that Washington expects South Korea to follow the alliance's decades-old standards, particularly in intelligence and counterintelligence, but did not say what standards were meant or whether it believes South Korea violated them. A State Department official, asked about the report that the U.S. sought the NIS's exclusion, reportedly said the U.S. expects all allies to handle intelligence and counterintelligence matters appropriately (citing Voice of America).
- An NIS official reportedly acknowledged on Oct. 8 that the NIS was left out of the June 25–26 meeting at the U.S.'s request. Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff Jin Young-seung (진영승) reportedly told the defense committee's audit on Oct. 8 that some key North Korean nuclear intelligence is being restricted but that nuclear and missile response missions are unaffected, adding "it would be best for this situation to be resolved quickly." National Security Adviser Wi Sung-lac (위성락) reportedly acknowledged in April that matters about Kusong are combined South Korea–U.S. secrets, while explaining that the government's position is that Minister Chung did not leak information received from the U.S.
- The @issue_bongbong caption largely relays the U.S. official's remark and the reports above, but its thumbnail — "Trump warns the South Korean government / Are you trying to break the alliance with us now? / We can't trust Korea" — is not confirmed as anything President Trump said. The reported speaker was an unnamed senior administration official, and we did not find "break the alliance" or "can't trust" in reports.

- An [Instagram reel by @now.new.mag](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DeThmyZy73e/) (Oct. 10) presenting the same issue as "allegations the NIS 'refused to participate'" (thumbnail: "Is the NIS on North Korea's side too? / The U.S. proposed a meeting to stop North Korean hacking and it refused") is also noted here. According to reports, the NIS did not refuse; **the U.S. asked for the NIS's exclusion and the NIS was left out of the meeting** (as an NIS official acknowledged). "Is the NIS on North Korea's side too?" is an unfounded insinuation and is not included in the summary.

- An [Instagram post by @monarchreport25](https://www.instagram.com/p/DePJPErDT3_/) presenting the same issue in English (Oct. 8, "The U.S. said no to South Korea's top spy agency") is also noted here. Its account that the U.S. asked to keep the NIS out and that the NIS missed the June meeting for the first time matches the reports above.

## Remarks by Minister Chung and Ambassador Kang on Oct. 12 (update)

- An [Instagram reel by @got_dem_catch](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DeavjLBzNKG/) (Oct. 13, "Chung Dong-young says the alliance is fine, but Kang Kyung-wha can't even communicate with the White House?") points to discord within the government's security team and asks whether concrete steps to restore South Korea–U.S. intelligence cooperation are needed more than a blame game (the poster's opinion). It is noted here instead of in a separate post.
- At an Oct. 12 press meeting, Minister Chung Dong-young (정동영) reportedly said "there is nothing wrong with the South Korea–U.S. alliance" and rejected the U.S. view that Chung's remark about a "North Korean nuclear facility in Kusong" leaked classified information provided by the U.S., calling it a "misunderstanding" and saying "the U.S. is wrong." Chung reportedly explained that the facility had been mentioned publicly since the July 2025 confirmation hearing.
- The same day (local time), at the parliamentary audit of the embassy in Washington, Ambassador to the U.S. Kang Kyung-wha (강경화) reportedly named intelligence cooperation as the hardest issue between the two countries, said the U.S. raised it directly at the Sept. 18 meeting between Foreign Minister Cho Hyun and Secretary of State Marco Rubio, and said "I don't think the problem has yet been resolved as much as the U.S. expects." Kang also reportedly said "I admit there are limits to communication with the White House" and that high-level consultations are not happening "to the point that even I find it frustrating," and confirmed relaying Minister Chung's explanation to U.S. officials after a phone call from Chung. Kang also said support for the alliance in Washington and the bond between the two countries remain firm.
- On the North Korean mine explosion, Minister Chung has kept a cautious position that it cannot yet be determined whether it was a deliberate attack, while Ambassador Kang reportedly answered that it was "clearly not an unforeseen accident" and that "it is clear North Korea laid them."
- After the ambassador's remarks, the People Power Party and other opposition figures reportedly demanded the immediate dismissal of Minister Chung and NIS Director Lee Jong-seok (이종석).

## What we left out of the summary

- That the U.S. asked for the exclusion over classified-information security is, even in the report, a sources' account phrased as "apparently" and "reportedly"; we did not find an official U.S. government statement.
- The NIS statement reportedly said only that "there was some misunderstanding," without specifically confirming or denying the exclusion request or its absence from the meeting.
