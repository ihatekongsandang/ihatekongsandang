This post is based on the original caption; only the first card image was reviewed. The original cites Munhwa Ilbo, Newsis, Maeil Shinmun, MBN, YTN and KBS reports as its sources. Related posts (some may be available only in Korean): [petition to end visa-free entry for Chinese nationals](/post/2026-09-16-pickmag-china-visa-free-petition).

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- Even as reports of crimes by Chinese nationals continue, the government and local authorities are focused on attracting Chinese tourists; if more tourists are to be drawn, entry screening, stay management and crime-prevention measures should come first. Public safety comes before tourism revenue.

## What reports confirm

- On Sept. 21, at a lodging in Seogwipo, Jeju, five Chinese men allegedly confined two Chinese women in their 20s, took their phones and other belongings, and forced them to transfer 100,000 yuan (about 20 million won); police reportedly arrested three of the men on robbery and confinement charges, and are tracking the other two, who had already left the country, through Interpol cooperation. The investigation reportedly began after one victim escaped and sought help.
- The Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism and North Gyeongsang Province reportedly held a Korea-China tourism exchange event in Andong on Sept. 11-12 with Chinese travel agencies and influencers, discussing tour packages linked to regional airports.

## What we left out of the summary

The casino assault-and-extortion case (Maeil Shinmun) and the unmanned-store theft case (MBN) that the original also introduced were reported to be at the investigation/booking stage, and we did not separately check their original articles this time. The victims in the Jeju case are also Chinese nationals.
