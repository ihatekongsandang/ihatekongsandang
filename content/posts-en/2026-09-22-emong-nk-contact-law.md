## What reports confirm

- The Ministry of Unification reportedly decided to accept an amendment to the Inter-Korean Exchange and Cooperation Act sponsored by Rebuilding Korea Party Rep. Kim Joon-hyung (김준형). The core of the amendment is the deletion of Paragraph 3 of Article 9-2, a provision reported to require anyone wishing to contact North Korean residents to report it in advance to the unification minister and to allow the minister to refuse to accept the report.
- The amendment would reportedly remove the power of refusal and require only a results report to be submitted, with a fine for failing to submit one.
- The Ministry of Justice and the National Intelligence Service reportedly take the same position.
- The number of cases in which the ministry refused advance contact reports under this provision was reported as 4 in 2022, 44 in 2023, 25 in 2024 and 16 in 2025.
- If passed, the contact-restriction provision would reportedly disappear 36 years after the Inter-Korean Exchange and Cooperation Act was enacted in 1990.
