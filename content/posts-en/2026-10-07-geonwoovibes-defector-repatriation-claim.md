This post is based on the original caption and the first frame of the reel (we did not review the whole video). The first frame's "Forced repatriation is funny?" differs in wording from Park's reported remark ("Is this a funny matter right now?"), so we used a card made by this site instead of the original image. The tip is Park's claim and has not been confirmed as fact.

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says lawmaker Park Choong-kwon (박충권) told the parliamentary audit that a tip had come in that the unification ministry's settlement support division and Minbyun (Lawyers for a Democratic Society, 민변) were contacting defectors settled in South Korea and coaxing them to return north, asking whether they wanted to go back, and that when Unification Minister Chung Dong-young (정동영) and some lawmakers laughed, Park protested, "Is this a funny matter?"
- Noting that the claim rests on a tip and that the ministry denied it as groundless, it argues that "it should not be laughed off — a thorough investigation must come first," and that "if true, someone must be held responsible; if not, it must be made clear."

## What reports confirm

- At the National Assembly foreign affairs and unification committee's audit of the unification ministry on Oct. 7, People Power Party lawmaker Park reportedly said a tip had come in that "the ministry's settlement support division and Minbyun have joined hands to keep contacting defectors who are settled and living well in South Korea, coaxing them to return north along the lines of 'don't you want to go back to the North?'" Park reportedly said 11 defectors were contacted and 2–3 of them persuaded, and that an official involved had been accused and was on trial at first instance.
- When Minister Chung and some Democratic lawmakers laughed during the questioning, Park reportedly protested, "Please don't laugh," and "Is this a funny matter right now? I'm talking about something serious." Ruling-party floor manager Hong Ki-won (홍기원) of the Democratic Party reportedly said the laughter came "naturally because the content didn't fit common sense," Democratic lawmaker Jung Chung-rae (정청래) said "basic physiological reactions are hard to control," and opposition floor manager Kim Dae-sik (김대식) of the People Power Party asked colleagues to "show respect and courtesy." Committee chair Song Seok-jun (송석준) reportedly told the Democratic side to "please observe courtesy."
- Asked whether anyone had coaxed or threatened the two North Korean prisoners of war brought to South Korea to return north, Minister Chung reportedly replied, "Such a thing cannot happen."
- After the audit, the ministry reportedly issued an official statement calling the claim "completely groundless," saying its division had in the past interviewed some defectors to check how they were settling in but that the "coaxing to return north" claim is untrue, and that the person concerned has filed a state compensation suit against the government, which is under way, so the facts will be established in court.

## What we left out of the summary

- Whether the ministry and Minbyun actually coaxed anyone to return north was not confirmed in reports. Park made the claim based on a tip, the ministry denied it, and a related lawsuit is reportedly under way. Outlets reported the division's name differently, as the settlement support division and the policy support division.
- We did not find Minbyun's position in the reports we checked.
