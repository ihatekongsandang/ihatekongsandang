## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It claims that the North Korean regime and military — which threaten South Korea with nuclear weapons and lay mines that leave its people with feet amputated at the ankle — are South Korea's main enemy.
- It argues that under the Constitution, North Korea is not a state but an anti-state organization illegally occupying South Korean territory, and criticizes that failing to say this clearly, or recognizing North Korea as a state, is a violation of the Constitution and an anti-state act that disqualifies a person from public office.

## What reports confirm

- First slide: At a KBS presidential candidates' debate on April 19, 2017, when candidate Yoo Seung-min (유승민) asked, "Is North Korea the main enemy?", candidate Moon Jae-in (문재인) replied, "I don't think that is something to say as a president." Moon later explained that North Korea is clearly a military threat and also, under the Constitution, a partner for peaceful unification.
- At confirmation hearings in July 2025, Unification Minister nominee Chung Dong-young (정동영) reportedly answered "I do not agree" when asked whether North Korea is the main enemy, and Labor Minister nominee Kim Young-hoon (김영훈) reportedly agreed with this, then after a back-and-forth answered, "Yes, it is the main enemy."

## Answer on Cheonan and Yeonpyeong (update)

- The @todaipick reel presents "unification minister nominee Chung Dong-young's confirmation hearing remarks" as if they were recent, but the hearing was held on July 14, 2025, and Chung Dong-young (정동영) was subsequently appointed and is serving as unification minister.
- At the hearing, nominee Chung reportedly said "North Korea's response changed when the Lee Myung-bak government reversed 10 years of the Kim Dae-jung and Roh Moo-hyun governments' inter-Korean reconciliation policy," and answered "yes" when a lawmaker asked, "Do you mean the Lee Myung-bak government's hard-line policy partly provided a cause for the 2010 sinking of the Cheonan and the shelling of Yeonpyeong?" Asked whether North Korea is the main enemy, Chung reportedly answered that it is a "threat" rather than the main enemy.
- The thumbnail's "Cheonan and Yeonpyeong shelling were our fault too" is the poster's summary of that answer; the reported answer was agreement with a question about whether the Lee Myung-bak government's hard-line policy "partly provided a cause." "Which country's minister is this?" is the poster's opinion. We did not review the reel's video and checked only its caption and thumbnail.
