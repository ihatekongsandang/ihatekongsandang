## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It claims that the North Korean regime and military — which threaten South Korea with nuclear weapons and lay mines that leave its people with feet amputated at the ankle — are South Korea's main enemy.
- It argues that under the Constitution, North Korea is not a state but an anti-state organization illegally occupying South Korean territory, and criticizes that failing to say this clearly, or recognizing North Korea as a state, is a violation of the Constitution and an anti-state act that disqualifies a person from public office.

## What reports confirm

- First slide: At a KBS presidential candidates' debate on April 19, 2017, when candidate Yoo Seung-min (유승민) asked, "Is North Korea the main enemy?", candidate Moon Jae-in (문재인) replied, "I don't think that is something to say as a president." Moon later explained that North Korea is clearly a military threat and also, under the Constitution, a partner for peaceful unification.
- At confirmation hearings in July 2025, Unification Minister nominee Chung Dong-young (정동영) reportedly answered "I do not agree" when asked whether North Korea is the main enemy, and Labor Minister nominee Kim Young-hoon (김영훈) reportedly said he agreed with this, then after a back-and-forth answered, "Yes, it is the main enemy."

- The politicians and remarks on the second slide onward could not be checked, so we did not cover them. The assessment that they are "unfit for public office" is the poster's opinion.
