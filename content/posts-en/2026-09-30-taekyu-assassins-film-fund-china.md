This post is based on the original caption and first slide; it is a multi-slide post of which only the first slide was reviewed. The original says the full commentary is on the People Power Party website, which we did not check directly; we compared its content with reports of Kim's press conference at the National Assembly the same day. Earlier controversy over the film (some may be available only in Korean): [Lee Jin-sook (이진숙): 'Assassin(s)' implies Park Chung-hee was behind the killing](/post/2026-09-29-leejinsook-assassins-film-distortion).

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It criticizes a double standard, saying the May 18 Democratic Uprising is treated as sacrosanct while the assassination of First Lady Yuk Young-soo (육영수) is turned into a conspiracy theory.
- It claims that huge taxpayer money was belatedly put into a film that had already finished shooting, that there are suspicions of "Chinese state capital" flowing into the producer, and that the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism is staying silent.
- It condemns the cultural sector for "filling its poverty of imagination with historical distortion" and the authorities for staying silent.

## What reports confirm

- At a Sept. 30 press conference at the National Assembly, Rep. Kim Tae-gyu (김태규) reportedly said government money the culture ministry had invested in the film account of the Korea Fund of Funds went into "Assassin(s)" through a private film investment fund. According to him, 34 billion won — 60% of a 56.7 billion won fund formed in July by Michigan Venture Capital — came from that film account, and the fund invested in "Assassin(s)" in August.
- Reports noted, however, that **this does not mean the entire 34 billion won went into a single film**. How much was actually invested in this film was not confirmed in the reports.
- Kim reportedly questioned the timing, saying the producer Hive Media Corp's audit report showed 13.2 billion won in production costs had already been spent by the end of last year, when filming ended, while the fund was formed only this July, half a year later.
- On the Chinese capital allegation, Kim reportedly said there had been a report that CLSA Capital, a private equity fund under the Chinese state-owned financial group CITIC, pursued a 40 billion won investment in the company in October 2023, and that a transaction of the same structure (including about 19 billion won in convertible bonds) appeared in the corporate registry this July. He reportedly said, "The registry does not show the investor's name, but we cannot rule out that it is money from the same Chinese private equity fund."
- Hive Media Corp reportedly said in a Sept. 29 statement that "there has been no inflow of Chinese capital at all" and that the film was "independently planned and produced with domestic capital."
- Kim reportedly said he would push for a special act against historical distortion that would also hold people accountable for distorting tragedies of modern Korean history, including the Korean War.

## What we left out of the summary

- The "inflow of Chinese state capital" is Kim's own inference — he said the possibility "cannot be ruled out" — and reports do not confirm that the investor was Chinese. The producer denied it.
- We could not find the culture ministry's position in the reports we checked.
