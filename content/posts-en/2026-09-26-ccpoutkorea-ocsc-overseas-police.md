This post is based on the original caption, and we used a card made by this site instead of the original image. The original was posted in September 2026, but it covers allegations reported in late 2022 and early 2023. The restaurant operator and others are not named.

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says an OCSC (Overseas Chinese Service Center), a type of organization China's State Council overseas Chinese affairs system encouraged to be set up, did exist in Seoul; that the Seoul OCSC held "one-day consulate" events with the Chinese Embassy, reportedly attended by South Korean police and justice ministry officials; and that the de facto operator of a Seoul Chinese restaurant said they headed the Seoul OCSC and had helped about 10 Chinese nationals return home.
- It introduces the human rights group Safeguard Defenders' claim that overseas outposts linked to Chinese local public security bureaus were involved in "persuading returns," while distinguishing that public information alone cannot establish that the Seoul OCSC or the restaurant was a secret police station. It argues the key questions are how far an organization linked to a foreign government operated in South Korea and whether the government oversaw it.

## What reports confirm

- According to December 2022 reports, the Seoul OCSC, accused of being a Chinese overseas "secret police station," held "one-day consulate" events from 2017 until the COVID-19 outbreak, and officials of the South Korean National Police Agency and justice ministry attended events in May and December 2017 and October 2018 (at the restaurant under suspicion) along with Chinese Embassy consular staff. The OCSC is described as a nonprofit type of organization that China's State Council Overseas Chinese Affairs Office has encouraged since 2014 to support Chinese living abroad.
- The international human rights group Safeguard Defenders reportedly said some OCSCs shared addresses with and were closely linked to Chinese secret police stations. The restaurant's operator reportedly denied the allegations at a December 2022 press conference, saying they had only helped about 10 Chinese nationals who were dead, injured or in difficulty return home on humanitarian grounds.
- The government reportedly said in January 2023 that it was checking the facts.

## What we left out of the summary

- That the Seoul OCSC or the restaurant was a Chinese "secret police station" has not been confirmed by any public investigation finding or ruling (the original caption makes the same distinction). The restaurant operator was later reportedly investigated on separate charges such as embezzlement, but this post could not confirm any conclusion on the secret police station allegation itself.
