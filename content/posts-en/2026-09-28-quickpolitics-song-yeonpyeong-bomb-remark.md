## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It introduces the controversy from right after North Korea's 2010 shelling of Yeonpyeong Island, when then-Incheon mayor Song Young-gil (송영길) visited the site and, holding up a scorched soju bottle, said, "This really is a bomb drink" (a pun: "bomb drink" is Korean slang for a shot of liquor dropped into beer).
- It notes that conservatives called it an inappropriate joke and even demanded his resignation, the Democratic Party countered that the context had been distorted, and that Song himself dismissed it as "petty nitpicking."

## What reports confirm

- After North Korea's shelling of Yeonpyeong Island in November 2010, Incheon mayor Song Young-gil visited the site and was reported to have said, holding up a scorched soju bottle in front of a damaged shop, "This really is a bomb drink," sparking controversy.
- The Grand National Party reportedly denounced it, saying, "This is unimaginable as a remark made at the scene of a brutal enemy attack," while the Democratic Party reportedly said the intent of the remark had been distorted.
- Mayor Song had also reportedly drawn criticism earlier for a Twitter post suggesting the Yeonpyeong shelling was provoked by a South Korean military exercise.

## What we left out of the summary

- We could not find Mayor Song's "petty nitpicking" rebuttal in the reports we checked, so it is presented here only as the original's claim. The "haha" in the thumbnail does not appear in the reported remarks.
