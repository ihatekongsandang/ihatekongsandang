## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says that, after Chinese men who sprayed paint and damaged property at a "Xi Jinping" Chinese restaurant left the country right after the crime, a similar incident in Jeju in June is drawing renewed attention.
- It says that on June 2, Falun Gong practitioners raising awareness of the Chinese Communist Party's persecution of Falun Gong in front of the Shilla Duty Free store in Jeju were attacked by three Chinese men, that a display board was damaged, and that two practitioners — one Korean and one Taiwanese — were injured.
- It says the men went to the police voluntarily for questioning but were said to have left for China the next day.
- It says a Falun Gong practitioner was also assaulted in front of the Chinese Consulate General in Toronto, Canada, on Sept. 22, and relays concern that the Chinese Communist Party's human rights abuses are spreading abroad.

## What reports confirm

- **The Jeju case**: Jeju Seobu Police Station reportedly said on June 4 that it had booked three Chinese men, including one in his 30s, for violating the Assembly and Demonstration Act by damaging rally materials such as display panels at a Falun Gong rally near the Shilla Duty Free store in Yeon-dong, Jeju City, at around 6 p.m. on June 2. They were reportedly stopped at the scene by police and accompanied officers voluntarily to a nearby police box, and police reportedly found they acted in anger after seeing Falun Gong materials and posts criticizing the Chinese government at the rally. In the process, the rally organizer reportedly said they had been assaulted and was taken to a hospital by 119 paramedics.
- **The Bundang case**: Bundang Police Station in Gyeonggi Province reportedly said on Sept. 27 that it had identified four Chinese men in their 20s to 40s suspected of breaking into a Chinese restaurant in Bundang-gu, Seongnam, at around 2:40 a.m. on Sept. 24, painting the interior with spray paint and paint, and spraying an unidentified substance presumed to be pesticide on food ingredients (charges including joint property damage). They reportedly left for China through Incheon International Airport after the crime, and police reportedly plan to pursue them through international judicial cooperation. The restaurant reportedly uses the name of China's top leader as its business name, and the motive was reportedly not confirmed.

## What we left out of the summary

- The specific circumstances of the two practitioners' injuries in the Jeju case, the remark attributed to an attacker, and the claim that the attackers left for China the next day were not confirmed in the general news reports we checked. The charge confirmed by reports is a booking under the Assembly and Demonstration Act over damaged rally materials; reports do not say whether assault charges were applied.
- The Sept. 22 incident in Toronto, Canada, was not confirmed by Korean or international news reports.
- The line that "the Chinese Communist Party's human rights abuses are spreading beyond its borders" is a concern relayed by the poster; reports did not establish any link between the two cases.
- The people identified as attackers are private individuals whose identities have not been made public, so the original video frames showing faces were not used.
