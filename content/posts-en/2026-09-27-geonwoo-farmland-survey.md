This post is based on the original caption; the video content was not reviewed. A [follow-up reel](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dd6KJN5TUdC/) the same account posted on Sept. 30 is noted here instead of in a separate post. It worries that, depending on the survey results, tenant farmers may have to give their land back and that a surge in listings could lower farmland prices and the collateral value held by farmers and agricultural cooperatives; it criticizes fiscal spending such as consumption coupons and urges farmers to make their voices heard in front of the presidential office, even by driving tractors there (the poster's opinion).

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It argues that the 27% flagged as suspected violations are suspected cases, not confirmed ones, and in the field there are concerns about a lack of advance publicity, unclear survey criteria, and falling farmland transactions and prices.
- It says that if elderly farmers and inherited farmland are not covered, the system should have been designed with rural realities in mind from the start.
- It warns that if the state excessively controls individuals' ownership and use of land, the property rights and private-property system guaranteed by the Constitution could be shaken.

## What reports confirm

- On Sept. 24 (Korea time), President Lee Jae-myung reportedly shared a post by Minister of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs Song Mi-ryung (송미령) on X, saying, "Every government has avoided this because of resistance and backlash, but I will not avoid it."
- In the same post, he reportedly said, "Cases where people farmed but can no longer do so because of old age, or inherited farmland, are not covered," and claimed about the backlash that "those speculating in farmland … often stir people up with lies" and that "once approval ratings fall, reform becomes increasingly difficult."
- The nationwide farmland survey was reportedly pursued on the president's instruction in February 2026, and after an amendment to the Farmland Act passed the National Assembly on May 7, the survey of 1.36 million hectares of farmland acquired on or after Jan. 1, 1996, reportedly began on May 18.
- In the basic survey as of the end of July, 2.84 million parcels — 27.0% of those covered — were reportedly classified as suspected violations such as illegal leasing, unauthorized fallowing or illegal conversion, and reports said a suspected classification alone does not immediately lead to a disposal order.
- At a government–Democratic Party consultation on Sept. 21, the two sides reportedly announced supplementary measures to deal strictly with clear speculation while giving cases of inheritance, aging and customary leasing a chance to be regularized; the People Power Party reportedly criticized this as "rushing ahead first, cleaning up later," pointing to shrinking farmland prices and transactions.

## What we left out of the summary

The field concern in the original that "farmland transactions have dropped sharply and prices are falling" was reported as part of the partisan dispute, but we could not confirm it with transaction-volume or price statistics. The follow-up reel's concerns about tenant farmers returning land and falling collateral value are also the poster's projections; as noted above, the government and ruling party reportedly offered supplementary measures giving customary tenancies a chance to be regularized.
