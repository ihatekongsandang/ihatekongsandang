## What reports confirm

- NewsVerse reported on Sept. 28, citing multiple government sources, that the National Forensic Service's first analysis had tentatively concluded the blast was presumed to be a North Korean mine explosion; the JCS reportedly said the same day that it "has never officially been notified of the analysis results by the NFS."
- Troops at the scene reportedly testified, from right after the blast, that they had seen a North Korean box mine, and testimony also reportedly emerged that there was one more unexploded mine in addition to the one that went off.
- Independent lawmaker Han Dong-hoon (한동훈) reportedly argued that the investigation was delayed even though troops had testified since the 21st that it was a North Korean mine, suggesting the government and military leadership put off the investigation out of deference to North Korea.
- Reports noted that South Korea's M14 anti-personnel mine carries about 28 g of TNT, versus some 200-odd g for a North Korean box mine, a large difference in destructive power.

## What we left out of the summary

Reports of the NFS analysis results cite anonymous sources, and there has been no official announcement. Rep. Han Dong-hoon's "deferring to North Korea" claim is a political assertion, and the JCS has disputed the claim of a delayed investigation.
