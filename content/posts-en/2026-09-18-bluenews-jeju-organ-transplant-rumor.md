This post is based on the original caption and first slide; it is a multi-slide post of which only the first slide was reviewed. A post on a Jeju missing-persons case from the same period: [Police officer arrested for falsely closing missing-person reports in Jeju](/post/2026-08-24-dictatorshipnono-jeju-missing-false-closure).

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It introduces a Chinese-language text claiming that Chinese people can enter Jeju visa-free and complete organ transplant surgery and recovery in one place.
- It notes, however, that the image gives no hospital name or official source, so whether such a service actually exists was not confirmed, and that because deceased-donor transplants in Korea go through recipient selection by the Korea Network for Organ Sharing under set criteria, visa-free entry alone does not guarantee a transplant.
- It concludes that the capture alone does not establish that illegal organ trading or any crime took place.

## What reports confirm

- After missing persons were found dead one after another in Jeju in August, baseless rumors such as "links to a Chinese organ-trafficking ring" reportedly spread online, and on Aug. 26 Jeju Province reportedly expressed "deep regret that unconfirmed facts and exaggerated interpretations are being circulated," asking people to refrain from spreading such content and to delete incorrect content.
- Danbi News (단비뉴스) reported on Oct. 1 that the Chinese-language text was posted to an online community on Aug. 28 with no identifiable source. Jeju's visa-free program lets foreigners stay up to 30 days, limited to Jeju; to register on the waiting list for a deceased-donor transplant, foreigners must have stayed in Korea at least one year under Korea Network for Organ Sharing (KONOS) guidelines, so visa-free entrants reportedly cannot meet the registration requirement at all.
- The same report relayed medical staff's explanation that living-donor transplants for foreigners are allowed only between relatives within the fourth degree and require notarized, consulate-certified home-country documents and KONOS approval, making it practically impossible within 30 days. It added, however, that a KONOS official said there had been a case in the past three years of approving a foreigner's documents within 10 days, so it could not be stated definitively whether a living-donor transplant within 30 days is possible. Jeju's two designated transplant hospitals, Jeju Halla Hospital and Jeju National University Hospital, reportedly confirmed they have never performed an organ transplant on a foreign patient.

## What we left out of the summary

- The first-slide phrase "the level of Jeju's organ transplant market, as told by Chinese people" repeats an unsourced text; we found no report of an organ transplant "market" for Chinese nationals in Jeju.
- Reports did not state definitively whether a living-donor transplant is possible within 30 days.
