This post is based on the original caption and its three images (a photo, a capture of the Chosun Ilbo headline and a summary card). The first image's "The U.S. refused the NIS director's visit to a 'North Korea surveillance agency'" is the headline of a Chosun Ilbo exclusive, but the NIS has flatly denied it, so we used a card made by this site showing both sides instead of the original image. Related post (may be available only in Korean): [Report that the U.S. asked to exclude the NIS from a trilateral cyber working group](/post/2026-10-08-shindawit-us-nis-cyber-group-excluded).

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says NIS Director Lee Jong-seok (이종석)'s March trip to the U.S. fell through for lack of coordination with Washington, that a visit to and meetings at the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA) under the U.S. Defense Department reportedly did not happen, and that there is a view that the CIA sees the director as pro-China.
- It notes the NIS said "it is not true" and could not confirm the director's movements, and adds that several NIS agents involved in the Coupang response were put on an entry-restriction list and that the U.S. has reportedly limited intelligence sharing on North Korea since March.

## What reports confirm

- Citing a Washington source, Chosun Ilbo reported in an Oct. 8 exclusive that Lee sought to visit the NGA this spring but the U.S. government did not allow it, and that a meeting with a U.S. intelligence chief also did not materialize, so the March trip fell through. The source reportedly said "the U.S. did not deny entry or a visa; it let the visit and meeting requests fall through." The same report quoted another source as saying "as the CIA came to see Lee as 'pro-China,' signs emerged of the NIS being excluded from intelligence consultation meetings."
- The same report said the U.S. government reportedly put several NIS agents involved in the response to the Coupang data breach on a "U.S. entry-restriction list," and that the U.S. has reportedly been limiting intelligence sharing on North Korea since Unification Minister Chung Dong-young (정동영) mentioned a uranium enrichment facility in Kusong, North Pyongan Province. Asked by Chosun Ilbo at the time, the NIS reportedly said "it is not true" and "we cannot confirm the movements of the head of an intelligence agency."
- In an Oct. 8 statement, the NIS reportedly flatly denied the report as "false and untrue," saying "in March, amid the outbreak of war in the Middle East, the director was in no position at all to travel abroad" and "there was never any plan to visit the U.S. NGA," and that the report on agents involved in Coupang being put on an entry-restriction list was "not true at all." The NIS reportedly said it would consider strong measures including legal action and that "intelligence cooperation between countries is a highly sensitive security matter, so it is difficult to offer any specific explanation beyond 'it is not true.'"
- Earlier, at the foreign ministry audit on Oct. 6, independent lawmaker Han Dong-hoon (한동훈) claimed "the U.S. denied (Lee) entry, citing anti-American leanings and other issues"; Foreign Minister Cho Hyun (조현) replied "not as far as I know," and the NIS reportedly said on Oct. 7 that it was "not true at all."

## What we left out of the summary

- The trip falling through, the NGA visit not being allowed, the entry restrictions on agents and "the CIA sees Lee as pro-China" all come from reports citing anonymous sources; we found no official U.S. government confirmation. The NIS has flatly denied them.
- The original card's "NIS: 'Not true'… movements cannot be confirmed" was the NIS's answer to Chosun Ilbo's inquiry; the NIS later said in a separate statement that there was never any plan to visit the NGA.
