## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- @finf_media: At the UN General Assembly First Committee on Oct. 8, when Ambassador Cha Ji-hoon (차지훈) said the DMZ mine blast was caused by a mine deliberately laid by North Korean troops and called for an apology, a North Korean delegate took the right of reply and rebutted by citing Unification Minister Chung Dong-young (정동영)'s Oct. 7 audit remark ("it cannot yet be identified as a mine attack"), and the poster notes the minister's remark was used as grounds for the North's rebuttal.
- @swing_politics: It says Kim Yo-jong claimed in an Oct. 8 statement that "even South Korea's official Chung Dong-young" had "confessed" it was hard to conclude it was a deliberate North Korean attack, while noting that the minister's remark meant intent had not been determined, not that the North bore no responsibility.

## What reports confirm

- At the general debate of the First Committee (disarmament and international security) of the 81st UN General Assembly on Oct. 8 (local time), South Korean Ambassador to the UN Cha Ji-hoon reportedly said "a joint on-site investigation and forensic analysis by our military and the UN Command confirmed the blast was caused by a mine deliberately laid south of the Military Demarcation Line by North Korean troops," calling it a violation of the Armistice Agreement and urging a public apology, responsible measures and a response to dialogue. It was reportedly the first time the government named North Korea's responsibility for the incident at a formal UN meeting.
- The North Korean delegation took the right of reply, calling it "an impure obstruction aimed at distorting legitimate fortification of the southern border area as military aggression," and named Minister Chung directly: "Didn't even a South Korean official say it is hard to conclude this was a deliberate attack by the North?" it was reported. After South Korea's delegation rebutted again, the North reportedly took the floor again, defined South Korea as a hostile state and said "the exercise of military sovereignty within our territory is not something to discuss with South Korea."
- At the National Assembly foreign affairs and unification committee's audit on Oct. 7, Minister Chung reportedly said, in effect, that it was hard to identify the mine blast as a direct attack by North Korea. Kim Yo-jong, director of the Workers' Party General Affairs Department, also cited the remark in an Oct. 8 statement, according to reports.

## What we left out of the summary

- The minister's reported remark withheld judgment on intent (whether it was an attack); it did not deny who laid the mine, which the JCS concluded on Oct. 5 was a mine North Korea installed south of the MDL (the @swing_politics original makes the same point).
