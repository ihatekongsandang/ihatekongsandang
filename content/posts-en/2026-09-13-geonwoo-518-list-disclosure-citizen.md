This post is based on the original caption posted on Sept. 13, 2026; the video content was not reviewed. The citizen who spoke is a private individual, so their identity is not given. Related post (may be available only in Korean): [Rep. Lee Jin-sook (이진숙) calls for disclosure of May 18 merit records](/post/2026-09-13-geonwoo-leejinsook-518-merit-disclosure).

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It relays that a citizen who said they are a Democratic Party member and the child of a national merit holder said they hope their parent's dedication is proudly recognized, and that if there is nothing to hide, there is no reason the list of May 18 democratic merit holders cannot be disclosed.
- The poster demands disclosure from the government and the Democratic Party, saying that if nothing is wrong with the list it should be disclosed and verified, and that if unqualified people received taxpayer support, this should be investigated and those responsible held accountable.

## What reports confirm

- In 2020, in a suit filed by about 100 citizens against the Patriots and Veterans Affairs agency (보훈처) to overturn its refusal to disclose information, the Seoul Administrative Court ruled that nondisclosure was lawful, finding that the list of May 18 merit holders and their records of merit are personal information whose disclosure risks invading privacy, and that the current review procedures prevent fraudulent registration.
- On Aug. 31, 2026, People Power Party Rep. Lee Jin-sook reportedly said she would push to amend the law to create a basis for disclosing the names and records of May 18 merit holders and to make review by the Ministry of Patriots and Veterans Affairs mandatory at registration.

## What we left out of the summary

- Neither the original nor reports we could find contain a specific case of an unqualified person being registered as a merit holder and receiving support.
