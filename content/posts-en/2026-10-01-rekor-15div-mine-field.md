## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- Citing an MBN exclusive, it says that after the mine blast in the DMZ on the western front, signs have also been spotted that North Korean troops created a minefield south of the MDL in the area of the 15th Division on the central front.
- It says North Korean troops left digging traces about 20 meters north of the MDL early this year, were seen moving with boxes containing mines, and have been laying mines while crossing the MDL some 50 times over the past two months.
- It concludes that "the North Korean mine threat is not a problem of a few areas but is spreading into a security threat across the entire DMZ front."

## What reports confirm

- MBN reported on Oct. 1 that the Army's 15th Infantry Division reportedly identified traces of North Korean troops digging about 20 meters north of the MDL early this year. One military official reportedly said "North Korean troops were seen carrying boxes containing mines," which MBN presumed to be digging for mine-laying. Another official reportedly said North Korean troops crossed the MDL several times in that area last year alone.
- MBN reported that it is highly plausible the mine-laying work on the northern side continued across the MDL into the south. It said the 15th Division covers the central front around Cheorwon and Hwacheon in Gangwon Province, between the 25th Division on the western front, where the blast occurred, and the 21st Division on the eastern front.
- The same report said North Korea has been laying mines while crossing the MDL some 50 times over the past two months as part of its "border fortification," and that several presumed North Korean minefields south of the MDL have been detected by South Korean military satellites. The JCS and the UN Command reportedly completed their joint field investigation and plan to announce final results as early as next week.

## What we left out of the summary

- What reports confirm for the 15th Division area is digging traces and mine boxes being carried **north** of the MDL, and past MDL crossings. Whether mines were actually laid **south** of the MDL in this area was reported only as "signs" and "plausibility," without official confirmation by the military.
- The reports do not say where the "several" presumed minefields south of the MDL detected by satellite are located.
- The conclusion about "a security threat across the entire DMZ front" is the poster's assessment.
