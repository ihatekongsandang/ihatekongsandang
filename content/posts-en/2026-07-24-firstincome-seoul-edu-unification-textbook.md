## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It takes issue with the Seoul Metropolitan Office of Education's textbook *Peace and Unification Through Debate* for stating that "the North Korean nuclear problem worsened during confrontation and pressure, while nuclear development was halted or curbed during dialogue and negotiation," and for listing places North Korea uses for regime propaganda, such as Kim Jong-suk's former home and the Masikryong Ski Resort, as recommended tourist sites.
- It criticizes that the logic that stirred controversy in the 2022 textbook became the government's official North Korea policy in 2026 ("dialogue first, denuclearization next").
- It presents the arguments of both sides: supporters say "North Korea's nuclear program advanced further during periods when dialogue was cut off," while critics say "North Korea developed nuclear weapons even during periods of dialogue" and "abandoning CVID amounts to tacitly accepting North Korea's nukes."

## What reports confirm

- In December 2022, People Power Party Seoul city councilor Jung Ji-woong (정지웅) (Education Committee) reportedly criticized the peace and unification textbook submitted by the Seoul education office as "effectively defending the North Korean regime's nuclear tests," saying it contained statements on North Korea's nuclear program and a list of "North Korean tourist sites I want to visit," including Kim Jong-suk's former home and statue, performances by North Korean art troupes and the Masikryong Ski Resort. He cited as grounds that North Korea's first nuclear test took place in October 2006, under the Roh Moo-hyun administration.
- At a press conference on July 23, 2026, Unification Minister Chung Dong-young (정동영) reportedly said North Korea policy had shifted from "denuclearization first" to "peace first" since the Lee Jae-myung government took office, that the "denuclearization-first argument has effectively been abandoned," and that the goal had been redefined as "realizing a nuclear-free Korean Peninsula," with a strategy of first halting North Korea's nuclear program being pursued.

## What we left out of the summary

- Individual program names the original cited as 2022 budget cuts, and its analysis of "North Korea's current reality," such as barbed wire and mines along the MDL, releases from the Hwanggang Dam, the deployment of new self-propelled guns and the constitutional codification of nuclear use, could not be checked against reports for this post, so they were not included.
