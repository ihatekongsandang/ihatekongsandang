## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- The caption says the Democratic Party is pushing a law change that would remove the president and lawmakers from the investigative remit of the Corruption Investigation Office for High-ranking Officials (CIO, 공수처) and hand investigations of politicians to the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency (중대범죄수사청) under the Ministry of the Interior and Safety.
- It says the rationale is the CIO's poor record — about 14,000 cases received since its launch, 8 direct indictments and only 1 final conviction — and relays concern over whether investigations of the powerful can remain independent.
- The first frame reads "Democratic Party-led bill / President, Democratic lawmakers banned from investigation / can't be punished unless it's a violent crime."

## What reports confirm

- Democratic Party members of the National Assembly's Legislation and Judiciary Committee — Kim Ki-pyo (김기표), Kim Nam-hee (김남희), Kim Dong-a (김동아), Kim Han-kyu (김한규) and Park Gyun-taek (박균택) — reportedly plan to amend the CIO Act to narrow its remit to officials in judicial and investigative bodies, such as judges, prosecutors, police and agency investigators, holding a forum on an "overhaul of the CIO's functions" on Oct. 7 and starting the amendment after the parliamentary audit. Rep. Kim Ki-pyo reportedly said the bill is "at the draft-review stage" and that he plans to introduce it after the forum.
- The plan reportedly removes the president, prime minister and lawmakers from the CIO's remit, while broadening its scope for the remaining judicial officials from abuse of power and bribery to crimes in general, extending coverage to police and investigators of all ranks, and considering regional branch offices.
- The CIO reportedly indicted 8 of about 14,000 cases received since its 2021 launch, with 1 final conviction, and according to Rep. Kim Ki-pyo's office, 78% of cases opened from January 2023 to August this year exceeded the statutory processing deadline.
- Reports also noted that moving investigations of the president and lawmakers from the independent CIO to the interior ministry's agency could raise disputes over the neutrality and independence of investigations of politicians, and that opposition from the opposition party is a variable.

## What we left out of the summary

- **"President, Democratic lawmakers banned from investigation"**: The reported plan removes the president and lawmakers from the CIO's remit, with investigations of them handled by the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency. No report says investigating them would be banned, and the change covers all lawmakers, not only "Democratic lawmakers."
- **"Can't be punished unless it's a violent crime"**: We found no report supporting this. The reported expansion of investigative scope (to crimes in general) concerns the judicial officials who would remain under the CIO.
- The bill was reportedly still at the draft-review stage and had not yet been introduced.
- The phrases "strengthening privileges" and "invincible bulletproofing" in @got_dem_catch's post are the poster's assessment. @pinnews_1's post notes that "it is not yet law, and it is accurate to say the Democratic Party is pushing an amendment to exclude them, not that they have been excluded," consistent with reports.
