This post is based on the original caption and first slide; it is a multi-slide post of which only the first slide was reviewed. The original cites a Sept. 17 exclusive by New Daily (뉴데일리), a government Policy Briefing (정책브리핑) article (March 10) and a Chosun Biz (조선비즈) article (Feb. 9). We checked the New Daily report against the original article. We could not locate the Policy Briefing and Chosun Biz articles, so we checked the committee's establishment and makeup against other news reports instead.

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- Citing New Daily's exclusive, it says the presidential "Committee of Light" has been operating for more than half a year since its launch in a state where its own website and the list of its civilian members are hard to confirm publicly.
- It says close to 13 billion won is being put into the committee, including 4.851 billion won in reserve funds this year and 7.882 billion won in next year's budget.
- Citing materials from the office of People Power Party Rep. Kang Myung-gu (강명구), it says claims have been raised that many of the 20 civilian members come from civic groups linked to the rallies calling for former President Yoon Suk Yeol's (윤석열) impeachment, and that some have called for the impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae (조희대) and the abolition of the National Security Act.
- It relays criticism that, as a presidential body, the committee should transparently disclose its member selection criteria and spending.

## What reports confirm

- The government reportedly approved the "Regulation on the Establishment and Operation of the Committee of Light" at the Cabinet meeting on March 10, 2026. The committee is reportedly a presidential body honoring citizens who resisted the Dec. 3 martial law, made up of 10 government members and up to 25 appointed civilian members. Its main tasks reportedly include issuing "Certificates of Light" and gathering opinions on designating related national commemorative days.
- President Lee Jae-myung reportedly named Park Mi-kyung (박미경), board chair and co-president of a Gwangju environmental group (광주환경운동연합), as the committee's chair on June 26, and presented her letter of appointment at a citizens' event marking the committee's launch on July 17.
- In its Sept. 17 exclusive, New Daily reported that the committee had not set up its own website more than half a year after its launch and that its civilian member list was not easy for the public to find. It reported that 4.851 billion won in first-quarter reserve funds this year and 7.882 billion won for next year had been allotted to the committee, and that its term runs until March 31, 2028.
- The same report said materials obtained through the office of Rep. Kang (National Assembly Public Administration and Security Committee) showed 20 civilian members had been appointed, 15 of whom came from civic groups that led the rallies calling for former President Yoon's impeachment last year. It also reported that, according to meeting minutes it obtained, a subcommittee in August considered a plan for "honoring persons of merit," but the item was dropped, citing among other reasons that no one was killed or injured during the martial law.

## What we left out of the summary

- The "13 billion won" is the sum of this year's reserve-fund allocation and next year's budget allocation (about 12.7 billion won). These are budgeted amounts; actual spending was not confirmed in the reports.
- The reports do not say which date the original and New Daily used as the start of "more than half a year since its launch." The establishing regulation was reportedly approved on March 10, the chair named on June 26 and the launch event held on July 17.
- The names and backgrounds of individual civilian members were not reproduced. Characterizations of the members' group backgrounds (such as "from groups linked to the impeachment rallies") rest on New Daily and materials from Rep. Kang's office.
- We found no reported response or rebuttal from the committee or the government, and could not confirm whether a website was opened or the member list disclosed after the report.
