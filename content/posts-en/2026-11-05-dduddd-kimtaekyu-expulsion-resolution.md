## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- Labeled "[Breaking]," it says a resolution urging the expulsion of lawmaker Kim Tae-kyu (김태규) passed, led by the Democratic Party, with 166 votes in favor and one invalid, and that the People Power Party did not take part in the vote.
- It adds, "Are they really going all out now… is this democracy…?"

## What reports confirm

- A resolution urging the expulsion of People Power Party lawmaker Kim passed the National Assembly plenary session on Nov. 5, led by the Democratic Party; in a secret ballot, 166 of the 167 members present voted in favor and one ballot was invalid, according to reports. People Power Party lawmakers, including Kim, reportedly did not vote (OhmyNews).
- The resolution, introduced by Democratic lawmaker Cheon Jun-ho (천준호) and 159 others, says Kim violated lawmakers' duty to maintain dignity by denying the insurrection by former presidents Chun Doo-hwan (전두환) and Yoon Suk Yeol (윤석열), it was reported.
- The trigger was an appearance on JTBC's YouTube show on Sept. 30, where Kim reportedly answered "no" when asked whether Yoon's actions were insurrection and "I don't think so" when asked the same about Chun. At the Oct. 1 plenary session, Kim reportedly pushed back that critics were "nitpicking words to disparage the intent" (iNews24).
- The House Steering Committee passed the same resolution 16-0 on Oct. 2 after People Power Party members walked out, and Kim said in a statement that day that "even if that was not the true meaning, I regret having given grounds for misunderstanding," according to reports (The Fact, OhmyNews).
- A resolution urging expulsion is not legally binding; actual expulsion requires a disciplinary review by the National Assembly's Special Committee on Ethics and the approval of at least two-thirds of all members in a plenary vote, it was reported.

## What we left out of the summary

- "Going all out" and "is this democracy" are the poster's opinion. The original could read as if the vote itself expels Kim, but according to reports this was a resolution urging expulsion and does not affect Kim's seat. A personal insult directed at Kim by another lawmaker during the Oct. 1 plenary exchange is not reproduced.
