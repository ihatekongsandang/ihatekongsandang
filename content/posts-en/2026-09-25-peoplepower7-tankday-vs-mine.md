## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- Top: "This is the left." It says that over the Starbucks "Tank Day" controversy, President Lee said he would "never forgive it," which led to truth-finding demands and even a boycott.
- Bottom: It contrasts this with his attitude toward the North Korean mine provocation, describing it as "refusing a UN truth-finding probe," "not caring whether people live or die," and "[saying it] wasn't North Korea's doing."

## What reports confirm

- Starbucks Korea was criticized for naming a May 18, 2026 tumbler sales event "Tank Day," which evoked the tanks of the martial-law troops from the May 18 Gwangju events and the death of Park Jong-chul (박종철) under torture; President Lee strongly criticized it on social media, saying it was something no one "in human form" could do. Starbucks reportedly halted the event, apologized, and its CEO was dismissed; the boycott was reportedly organized by labor and civic groups.
- The site investigation into the DMZ mine accident (Sept. 21) was reportedly conducted jointly by the military and the UN Command six days after the accident, and the opposition raised suspicions that it was delayed out of consideration for the president's UN address. The presidential office reportedly said "the site investigation has nothing to do with the president's UN address at all, and was decided by the JCS and UNC as a military judgment," and the military explained that time was needed for treating the injured, forming the investigation team, and advance rehearsals with the UNC.
- The JCS announced on Sept. 28 that "the possibility of a North Korean mine is very high," and President Lee said on the 29th, "we will take the necessary measures according to the truth."

## What we left out of the summary

- The claim that "Lee refused a UN truth-finding probe" was not confirmed by reports. There is controversy over why the investigation was delayed, but no report says the UNC investigation itself was refused. "Not caring whether people live or die" is the poster's own phrasing.
