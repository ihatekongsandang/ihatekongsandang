## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says the UN Command announced that North Korea violated the Armistice Agreement because a North Korean mine was found south of the MDL.
- It says North Korean forces crossed the MDL, invaded the South and carried out military action, causing an unprecedented situation in which South Korean soldiers were injured by a North Korean mine.

## What reports confirm

- In a Sept. 30 statement, the United Nations Command reportedly said that during a joint site investigation with the South Korean military starting Sept. 27, it found one North Korean resin (plastic) anti-personnel mine buried south of the MDL, and that "based on the investigation results, the UNC determined that an Armistice violation occurred" and is "taking action through the established Armistice mechanisms."
- This was reportedly the first time the UNC had publicly judged the DMZ blast to be an Armistice violation.
- On Sept. 29, the JCS reportedly said it had identified another North Korean resin anti-personnel mine about 1 m behind the second blast crater, and reports said at least four North Korean mines had been confirmed within roughly 4 m of the blast site.
- Defense Minister Kang Shin-chul (강신철) reportedly said it was "extremely unlikely" that mines washed away by flooding would end up clustered in one spot like this, pointing to deliberate emplacement by North Korea.
- In a Sept. 30 statement, North Korea's Kim Yo-jong (김여정), a department director of the Workers' Party, reportedly called the incident "an obvious staged farce," claimed "our soldiers have never once crossed the border line," and warned of retaliation.

## What we left out of the summary

- As reported, the UNC statement based its finding of an Armistice violation on the discovery of a North Korean mine south of the MDL. It does not say that North Korean troops crossed the MDL to lay it, nor does it use words like "invasion" or "military action." The original's "invaded the South and carried out military action" is the poster's interpretation.
- When and how the mine was laid (for example, whether troops crossed the line to lay it directly) has not been officially concluded, based on reports.
