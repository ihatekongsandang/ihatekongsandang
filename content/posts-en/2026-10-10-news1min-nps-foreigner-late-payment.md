This post is based on the original caption and first image. The original caption reproduces an Edaily article, and the "social media post on the 30th" it mentions is from **Aug. 30** (the original was shared on Oct. 10). The first image's "don't discriminate by nationality" rewords the chairman's "There must be no discrimination between nationals and foreigners," so we used a card made by this site instead of the original image.

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- The first image reads "'Why support elderly Chinese?' — NPS chief responds to protests: 'don't discriminate by nationality.'"
- The caption (the Edaily article) says Chairman Kim said on social media that the late-payment system needs fixing but foreigners alone must not be discriminated against, and that in the first half of this year late-payment applications by ethnic Korean Chinese (792) and Chinese nationals (64) made up 80% of the total.

## What reports confirm

- National Pension Service (NPS) Chairman Kim Sung-joo (김성주) posted an article titled "The purpose, problems and fixes of the NPS late-payment system" on social media on Aug. 30, saying "a malicious frame is being spread as if one could work for a month and receive a pension for life," proposing fixes such as linking the late-payment period to the contribution period and a residence requirement, and stressing "there must be no discrimination between nationals and foreigners" and "no exclusion of specific countries," it was reported. On reciprocity, Kim reportedly said "methods that discriminate against foreigners by nationality require caution."
- A Health and Welfare Ministry survey found that three foreigners (including overseas Koreans) had paid one month of premiums and then 119 months late, and only one — an overseas Korean who actually lived in Korea throughout the late-payment period — receives an old-age pension, it was reported on Sept. 2.
- From Aug. 31, the NPS reportedly changed its guidelines so that the "period of residence in Korea" for foreigners' late payments is judged by actual residence (15 or more days a month), and on Sept. 1 the government and the NPS reportedly moved to amend regulations to extend reciprocity so that only nationals of countries that allow late payments for South Koreans living there can make late payments. Some reports noted the gap between this and the chairman's call for caution on reciprocity just two days earlier.

## What we left out of the summary

- Regarding the caption's "a Chinese national enrolled for just one month paid 119 months of late premiums at 60 and receives a monthly pension," reports said the only actual recipient found in the ministry's survey was one overseas Korean who actually lived in Korea.
- Although the original was shared in October, it does not mention that the government had since tightened late-payment requirements and moved to extend reciprocity.
