Other posts on this issue (some may be available only in Korean): [report on troops' eyewitness testimony](/post/2026-09-27-rekor-dmz-mine-testimony) · [report on the first forensic analysis](/post/2026-09-28-yukdaejeon-nisi-nk-mine).

## What reports confirm

- At a Sept. 28 Defense Ministry regular briefing, JCS Operations Division 1 chief Hwang Ju-bong (황주봉) reportedly said a member of the operation team had testified to seeing a shape resembling a North Korean box mine near the blast site. Troops reportedly based their testimony on the object's shape and color.
- Another team member reportedly testified to having "seen an object presumed to be a different type of mine," so the testimony diverged, and the military reportedly said it would determine whether it was buried and the exact mine type through further site investigation.
- The military and UN Command reportedly could not reach the blast point during the site investigation on the 27th, and the National Forensic Service's analysis results had reportedly not yet been officially notified to the military.

## What we left out of the summary

The thumbnail caption "it was a North Korean box mine" is more definitive than the JCS announcement. The JCS only confirmed "testimony of seeing a similar object" and said it had not been confirmed whether it was actually a box mine.

## Later developments

On the evening of Sept. 28, 2026, the JCS announced interim findings saying "it is not our mine and very likely a North Korean mine" (without yet concluding whether it was recently laid or displaced). Details: [JCS interim findings](/post/2026-09-28-pagememo-jcs-interim-result).
