## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says a man in his 60s indicted for wiretapping fighter-jet radio communications near Gunsan and other locations is a former PLA officer who served 40 years at a Beijing signals-intelligence unit, holding the rank of zhongxiao (lieutenant colonel-equivalent), and that he wiretapped 13 times since 2024, funneling 13.9 TB of data to China.
- It introduces a man in his 40s, also a former PLA special-forces member, who around the same time recruited a USFK contact and extracted classified material, building rapport through meals and gifts to obtain training footage and classified data.
- It points out that detections of Chinese nationals photographing military facilities and wiretapping have been occurring one after another recently.

## What reports confirm

- The Suwon District Prosecutors' Office's Pyeongtaek branch reportedly indicted a Chinese man in his 60s (Mr. A) and a Chinese man in his 40s (Mr. B) in September on charges of aiding the enemy in general. Mr. A was reportedly found to be a former zhongxiao who served about 40 years at a PLA signals-intelligence unit, and Mr. B, an ethnic Korean-Chinese, was reportedly found to have a background with the PLA's special forces and the Public Security Bureau.
- Mr. A is accused of wiretapping military communications 13 times between March 2024 and August 2026 near the Gunsan, Osan and Gwangju air bases, Camp Humphreys in Pyeongtaek, and a naval base in Busan; prosecutors reportedly believe he installed receivers and antennas in hotel rooms and other locations to record communications between pilots and control towers, storing the recordings and frequency data on an external hard drive and taking them out to China.
- Mr. B, who ran a military-surplus store near Camp Humphreys, is reportedly accused of providing meals and gifts to an active-duty U.S. soldier and a Korean civilian defense employee he came to know there, obtaining training footage of Apache attack helicopters and F-35s and after-action review materials from the Eulji exercise, and leaking some of it via Chinese messaging and social media apps.
- The revised Criminal Act, which expands the scope of espionage charges from "enemy state" to "foreign state," took effect Sept. 13.

## What we left out of the summary

- The scale of data collected cited in the original (13.9 TB) does not appear in the reports we checked and is presented here only as the original's claim. The identities of the U.S. soldier, civilian employee and other private individuals who provided materials were not covered.
