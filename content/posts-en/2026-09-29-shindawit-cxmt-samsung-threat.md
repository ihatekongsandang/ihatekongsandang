## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says a statement has emerged in which a former CEO of Chinese memory maker CXMT pressured Korean engineers who came from Samsung Electronics to hand over information, saying, "If you don't bring it, I'll report you to the Korean government."
- It describes this as circumstances suggesting the leak of the 18nm DRAM process technology Samsung spent 1.6 trillion won to develop, and introduces testimony that "there was talk that China's government would cut off investment if [CXMT] couldn't achieve 18nm."
- It states that CXMT turned its first profit last year after nearly a decade of losses, becoming the world's fourth-largest DRAM maker, and that circumstances were confirmed showing it recruited large numbers of Korean talent by offering two to three times their existing salaries.

## What reports confirm

- According to a statement from a former Samsung Electronics executive who worked as head of equipment investment at CXMT, former CXMT CEO David Wang reportedly pressured a team of Korean employees between October and December 2016, saying, "Bring us all the Samsung Electronics PRP (process recipe) and equipment information you have," and "if you don't bring it, I'll report you to the Korean government."
- This former executive, who moved to CXMT after 28 years at Samsung Electronics, was reportedly indicted for illegally leaking and using the 18nm DRAM process technology Samsung spent 1.6 trillion won to develop as the world's first of its kind, and was sentenced to seven years in prison in the first-instance trial last April; he is reportedly now in an appeal trial.
- The case reportedly came to light after another former Samsung executive, who had served as CXMT's first head of development, was implicated in a separate case of leaking semiconductor equipment technology in 2022.

## What we left out of the summary

- CXMT's turn to profitability, its rise to the world's fourth-largest maker, and its offer of two to three times salary are presented only as the original's claims, since we could not cross-check them in the reports we found. We did not write the names of individuals, including the indicted former executive.
