## What reports confirm

- On Sept. 21, three officers were reportedly injured in a blast presumed to be a mine during a 25th Division DMZ search operation, and who laid it was reportedly under investigation.
- Earlier, on Sept. 14, the JCS reportedly said, regarding North Korean troop activity crossing the Military Demarcation Line in front-line areas, that it had "identified some terrain changes and is closely consulting with the UN Command on the possibility that mines were laid."
- A claim was raised that North Korea had crossed the Military Demarcation Line to create minefields at up to six locations in a single front-line division's area alone, but the JCS reportedly responded that "whether there are six such points cannot be confirmed for security reasons."

## What we left out of the summary

Among the original's nine points, claims such as "the U.S. reported the news instead because the government stayed silent," "this route had already been cleared of mines," and "military officers cannot possibly not know where the mines are" were not included in the summary because we could not find reports supporting them. The conclusion ("a security gap resulting from looking the other way") is the poster's own opinion.
