## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It argues that wariness of China is not a phenomenon of one political camp, saying that in a joint JoongAng Ilbo–EAI survey, 85.7% said China is a threat to South Korea, including 81.7% of Democratic Party supporters and 90.0% of People Power Party supporters.
- It relays that 73.7% had an unfavorable impression of China and trust stood at 15.9%, and that 44.1% cited the "Communist Party one-party system" as the reason for their negative view. It assesses that, combined with cyber-espionage by China-backed organizations, the Chinese Communist Party is being perceived as a real security risk.

## What reports confirm

- In a joint survey by the JoongAng Ilbo and the East Asia Institute (EAI) (reported in August 2026), 85.7% reportedly answered "threatening" when asked about the level of threat China poses to South Korea, with 81.7% of Democratic Party supporters and 90.0% of People Power Party supporters reportedly giving the same answer.
- In the same survey, trust in the United States was reportedly sharply divided by camp, at 33.6% among progressives and 62.5% among conservatives.

## What we left out of the summary

- Detailed figures such as 73.7% unfavorable, 15.9% trust and 44.1% citing the "Communist Party one-party system," as well as the cyber-threat analysis, could not be checked against the original reports for this post, so we relayed them only as the original post's claims.
