## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It relays that Mr. A, then an active-duty Army enlisted sergeant (병장), passed military secrets related to Korea–U.S. combined exercises, the UN Command and South Korean military training seven times at the request of a Chinese contact, and that his four-year prison sentence was finalized.
- It says the court found that the secrets had leaked to a Chinese intelligence organization, even though there was no direct evidence that his contact was a Chinese intelligence operative, and asks whether the military's security system — under which an active-duty soldier could access and take out such material — was adequate.

## What reports confirm

- The Second Division of the Supreme Court reportedly finalized the lower-court ruling sentencing Army enlisted sergeant Mr. A (23), indicted on charges of general aiding of the enemy and leaking military secrets under the Military Criminal Act, accepting bribes after improper conduct and violating the Information and Communications Network Act, to four years in prison, a fine of 20 million won and forfeiture of 19.07 million won.
- Investigators found that from August 2024 to February 2025, Mr. A received 88,000 yuan (about 17.26 million won) over seven occasions and handed over the latest material on Korea–U.S. combined exercises and the UN Command, as well as material on South Korean military-only training and Army movements, photographing the secrets with a wristwatch camera he received from his contact.
- The Seoul High Court, in the second-instance trial, reportedly recognized his intent to benefit the enemy and ruled that "his culpability is grave in that he committed the crime even though, having received security training, he knew well that he must not leak secrets."
