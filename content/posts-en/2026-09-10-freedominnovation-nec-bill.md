## What reports confirm

- Ten Democratic Party members of the National Assembly's Legislation and Judiciary Committee (including Rep. Kim Yong-min (김용민)) reportedly introduced on Sept. 8 an amendment deleting Paragraphs 1 and 2 of Article 5 of the Addenda to the NEC Special Counsel Act (the basis for dispatched prosecutors' investigative authority).
- People Power Party floor leader Jung Jeom-sik (정점식) reportedly criticized it as "a fraudulent act that neutralizes the special counsel law the ruling and opposition parties agreed on last July."

## Later developments (important)

The Democratic Party reportedly **withdrew the amendment on Sept. 10** after the controversy. Floor spokesperson Lee Ju-hee (이주희) reportedly said that "in the recent process of adjusting 140 bills all at once to fit the amended Criminal Procedure Act, the special counsel law amendment was drafted and introduced in a way that differed from the intent of the agreement," and that the party plans to reintroduce it so that the special counsel's dispatched prosecutors can investigate.

The original post predates the withdrawal.

## Later special counsel investigation (updated Oct. 12)

- The special counsel team on the National Election Commission led by Lee Tae-han (이태한), launched on Sept. 7, is investigating the ballot shortage in the June 3 local elections and related matters for up to 150 days and raided the National Election Commission on Sept. 17, it was reported. The team is pursuing an evidence check in which ballots stored at the Olympic Park Handball Gymnasium in Songpa-gu, Seoul (a counting site at the time) would be compared one by one in front of the media and the public; Oct. 29 is the likely date but may change depending on consultations with relevant agencies, according to reports. The team reportedly said the procedure is an evidence check, not a recount challenging the election result.
- An [Instagram post by @got_dem_catch](https://www.instagram.com/p/DeXAuj5k6YF/) (Oct. 12) summarizing this under the title "Will the truth about election fraud come out?" is noted here instead of in a separate post. The original caption itself says "no election fraud has been confirmed so far," and this site does not cover claims of election fraud, relaying only the special counsel's official procedure. The first image's photo of a "new election" placard and the line "Korea's election fraud truth revealed?" were not included in the summary.

- An [Instagram reel by @issue_target](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dedn0ipvevf/) (Oct. 14, "Joint investigation HQ finds signs of falsely entered turnout figures… raids the election commission") on the investigation by the joint prosecution-police investigation headquarters, before the special counsel was launched, is noted here as well. The reel presents it as recent news, but the raid took place on July 23, 2026: that day the headquarters raided the National Election Commission and the Songpa, Gangnam and Seocho district election commissions in Seoul, and said it had found signs that officials of the National Election Commission and the Gyeonggi Provincial Election Commission falsely entered turnout figures, also raiding those involved on charges of falsifying public electronic records and obstructing official duties, according to reports. The case was later handed to the special counsel launched in September. The original caption itself says "signs of altered computer figures do not immediately mean vote rigging or manipulation of election results," and the thumbnail's "Election commission manipulation suspicion caught / Young people's cries are being proven true" and rally photo were not included in the summary.

- On Oct. 15, the special counsel team simultaneously raided seven election commissions, including the National Election Commission, the South Chungcheong, South Gyeongsang and Gangwon provincial commissions, the South Jeolla-Gwangju commission, and the Busan Nam District and Jeju Seogwipo commissions, and said that, adding 25 to the five (including Seocho and Gangnam districts in Seoul) identified by the joint investigation headquarters, it had found signs that voter counts were falsely entered on election day at 30 city, county and district election commissions nationwide, according to reports. It explained that when voter counts wrongly entered for certain hours exceeded the final voter count, staff later altered the overall figures to make them look normal, or entered identical values or fixed increments, and that it had questioned some 70 officials in charge of real-time turnout reporting, it was reported. An [Instagram reel by @geonwoo.vibes](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DegBXbUzjb2/) (Oct. 15, citing Yonhap News TV footage) covering this is also noted here. What the special counsel disclosed are signs of false entries in hourly voter count tallies, not an announcement that final election results changed.

- On Oct. 28, the special counsel team carried out a public check of about 2.47 million ballots at the Olympic Park Handball Gymnasium, finishing more than 12 hours later at around 12:38 a.m. on Oct. 29, it was reported. The start was delayed by about three hours over whether observers could film on their own devices; filming by two broadcast cameras was allowed, and the National Forensic Service provided a digital microscope and an infrared spectrometer, according to reports. Special counsel Lee Tae-han reportedly said the team "accepted the voluntary submission of an entire box of certain ballots, respecting the observers' objections," and that it "selected a considerable number of ballots needing further analysis and plans to examine them in detail later." A [Threads post by @your_loie](https://www.threads.com/@your_loie/post/DfCjaQHiel6) (Oct. 28) relaying an observer's claim at the site that "the ballots in the first box are 100% fake" is also noted here. The original itself says this "has not been officially confirmed"; the special counsel said only that it selected ballots for detailed examination, and no finding on whether any were forged has been reported. The observer's claim is unconfirmed.
