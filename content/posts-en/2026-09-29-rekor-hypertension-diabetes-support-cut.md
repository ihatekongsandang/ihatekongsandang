This post is based on the original caption. It is a multi-slide post of which only the first slide was reviewed. The original cites Medical Newspaper (의학신문, Sept. 29, 2026) as its source.

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says the government is reorganizing its chronic-disease management program and pushing to end medical and drug-cost support for registered elderly patients with hypertension and diabetes.
- It notes that while the number of centers will grow from 19 to 29, the 5.21 billion won budget for patient medical-cost support will be cut entirely, and introduces experts' concern that the existing system should not be scaled back without first verifying the new program's effectiveness.

## What reports confirm

- The Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency is reportedly pushing a plan to end medical and drug-cost support under the hypertension/diabetes registration-management program starting in 2027 and reorganize it around education and counseling; the center-operation budget will reportedly rise from 3.28 billion to 4.71 billion won, while the 5.21 billion won patient medical-cost support budget will be cut entirely.
- Registered patients aged 65 and older have reportedly received 1,500 won in support per clinic visit and 2,000 won per prescription, and about 450,000 existing elderly registered patients could reportedly be affected.
- The medical community reportedly expressed concern that even a small amount serves as motivation for elderly, low-income chronic-disease patients to keep visiting hospitals regularly, and that the registration system could be destabilized; both ruling and opposition lawmakers reportedly raised concerns in the National Assembly about the increased medical-cost burden on the elderly.

## What we left out of the summary

- The photo of the president on the original's first slide is not a scene directly related to the program reorganization, so it was not explained here. The budget plan is at a stage before National Assembly deliberation.
