## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says a mine exploded on Sept. 21 in front of troops clearing a patrol route about 10 m from the MDL, and that the wounded had to dig an escape path themselves with hand picks amid the risk of more mines.
- It says it has not been finally confirmed whether the mine was a North Korean box mine, a South Korean mine or a displaced one, and asks which should be clarified first — the mine's origin or the safety measures at the site. It adds that one soldier lost a leg.

## What reports confirm

- The accident reportedly occurred as a soldier stepped forward while withdrawing during a route-clearing operation involving 18 troops about 10 m south of the MDL. After the first blast knocked down three soldiers, a second blast went off near the reconnaissance battalion commander as he approached to help, seriously injuring his left ankle. One officer's ankle was reportedly amputated.
- To get out safely over about 20 m after the explosions, combat engineers reportedly abandoned the standard detector method and knelt, carefully scraping the soil surface with hand picks to secure a withdrawal route.
- After the original was posted, the JCS announced on the night of Sept. 28 that "it is not our mine and very likely a North Korean mine," and on Sept. 29 said it had found one more North Korean resin anti-personnel mine at the site.

## What we left out of the summary

- The original says the "injured soldiers themselves" dug, but reports say the escape route was secured by combat engineers using hand picks. The original's "cause unconfirmed" has since been superseded by the JCS announcement.
