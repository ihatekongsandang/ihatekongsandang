This post is based on the original caption. It is a multi-slide post of which only the first slide was reviewed.

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It takes issue with a "respect in the barracks" lecture given to 1,600 trainees at the Army Training Center in Nonsan during UFS in August, saying Javier Bardem's remarks on "toxic masculinity" were used as teaching material and the presentation was filled with articles criticizing Trump, Putin and Netanyahu.
- It relays that the Army said, "As there is room for misunderstanding, we will stress the use of standard lesson plans," and that the lecturer said, "I didn't know it was during the UFS period."

## What reports confirm

- According to material People Power Party Rep. Park Sung-hoon (박성훈) obtained from the Ministry of National Defense, the Army Training Center reportedly entrusted a civilian lecturer with a lecture for 1,600 trainees from Aug. 20 to 27 on the theme "The power that leads a strong military! Barracks of trust and mutual respect," and the material reportedly featured a photo of Bardem and the phrase "Toxic masculinity is the cause of war."
- At a press conference at the Cannes Film Festival in May, Bardem had explained "toxic masculinity" and said, "It applies to Trump, Putin and Netanyahu as well."
- Rep. Park reportedly said that education criticizing the president of an allied country during a combined Korea–U.S. exercise blurs troops' sense of who the enemy is, and demanded an explanation of how it happened; the Army reportedly explained that it had merely used a news report as an example in education on gender sensitivity and a mutually respectful barracks culture, not to criticize a particular country's policies or diplomacy and security, and that it would stress making the fullest use of the Ministry of National Defense's standard lesson plans going forward.

## What we left out of the summary

- We did not cover the lecturer's identity or affiliated organization. The lecturer's remark "I didn't know it was during the UFS period" was not found in the reports we checked, so we relayed it only as the original post's claim.
