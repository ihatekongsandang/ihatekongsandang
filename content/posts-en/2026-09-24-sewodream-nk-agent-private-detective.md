This post is based on the original caption and the reel's thumbnail. The thumbnail's "A private-detective request turned out to be North Korean orders — the price was 660 million won" reads as if the detective job cost 660 million won, but the reported 660 million won was cryptocurrency the defendant received from the agent as operating funds, and the detective was paid 2.5 million won, so we used a card made by this site instead of the original image. The defendant and others involved are private individuals, so their names are not given.

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says a 43-year-old who ran a crypto-asset investment company, A, received orders from a North Korean agent via Telegram in 2021 and about 660 million won worth of cryptocurrency as operating funds, gathered information on anti-North defectors and active-duty officers, and hired a private detective to tail an active-duty captain under the pretext of checking on a daughter's boyfriend. It says the Seoul Central District Court sentenced A to 5 years in prison and 5 years' suspension of qualifications, and that A already has a separate 4-year sentence finalized.

## What reports confirm

- The Seoul Central District Court's Criminal Agreement Division 21 sentenced A (43), charged with espionage and other offenses under the National Security Act, to 5 years in prison and 5 years' suspension of qualifications, it was reported on Sept. 5.
- A was found to have received orders via Telegram from a North Korean agent in May–June 2021 to learn the personal details and activities of anti-North defectors, and around then to have received about 660 million won worth of cryptocurrency as operating funds for a crypto investment business, according to reports. A reportedly approached the head of a defector group offering to donate, passed the group's crypto wallet address to the agent, and approached a defector YouTuber who formerly worked for North Korea's State Security Department, suggesting "a glass of soju," to obtain a phone number.
- In July 2021, A reportedly received the names, units and resident registration numbers of seven active-duty officers from the agent with instructions to "check further information and recruit them." A reportedly obtained uniform and face photos of one captain from social media, then asked a private detective agency to tail the captain, saying "my daughter is seeing someone and I want to know about the person's relationships with women," paying about 2.5 million won in bitcoin; the agency reportedly reported the captain's unit, official residence, room number, schedule and movements. In August–September that year, A reportedly offered another officer money for information such as military organization charts but was turned down.
- The court reportedly found that photos, contact details and activities of anti-North defectors have real value worth protecting as secrets, because if known to the North they could be used to track or harm the defectors or retaliate against family members in the North. A was separately indicted for approaching an active-duty officer and leaking military secrets on the same agent's orders, and a sentence of 4 years in prison and 4 years' suspension of qualifications was reportedly finalized by the Supreme Court last December.

## What we left out of the summary

- The original's sentencing date of "Aug. 28" was not directly confirmed in the reports we checked (which said the sentence was handed down "recently" as of Sept. 5). As the original notes, this is a first-instance ruling; we could not confirm whether an appeal was filed.
- We did not find in the reports we checked the passage saying A claimed to have believed the counterpart was an ethnic Korean programmer living in China.
