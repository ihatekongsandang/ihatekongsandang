This post is based on the original caption; the video content was not reviewed.

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It argues that the National Security Act does not punish the mere fact of studying North Korea or Juche ideology; Article 7 punishes, under certain conditions, praising, encouraging or sympathizing with the activities of an anti-state organization, and producing or possessing materials benefiting the enemy.
- It says it should be questioned why he brought up Juche ideology and the National Security Act in front of overseas Koreans, and acts that threaten national security should not be lightly passed over in the name of freedom of thought.

## What reports confirm

- At a meeting with Korean residents in Mexico City on Sept. 24 (local time), President Lee Jae-myung reportedly referred to the 2004 debate over the National Security Act, saying, "The ruling and opposition parties had almost agreed that, since abolishing the National Security Act entirely was too much, we should delete at least a few of its toxic provisions," and "It ended up not happening because the other side opposed, saying abolishing it entirely was not acceptable." He reportedly said, in effect, that the opportunity to delete toxic provisions such as the crime of praising and encouraging was lost because hard-liners in the ruling party at the time insisted on full abolition.
- At the same event, he reportedly said, "I may be president, but recently I found the police were conducting a search-and-seizure because someone had studied North Korea's Juche ideology."
- The president reportedly said that "going too fast, too far and too hastily and meeting a backlash" is also an obstacle to reform, and some media outlets reportedly interpreted this as aimed at writer Yoo Si-min (유시민).
- Article 7 of the National Security Act provides for punishing acts of praising, encouraging, propagandizing for or sympathizing with the activities of anti-state organizations, and producing or possessing materials benefiting the enemy for such purposes.

## What we left out of the summary

Reports did not specify which investigation the president was referring to when he mentioned a "search-and-seizure because someone had studied Juche ideology," so we could not confirm it.
