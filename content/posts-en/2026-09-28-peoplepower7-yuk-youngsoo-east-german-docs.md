This post is based on the first image of the original post. The original caption is empty, and the image is a screenshot of an article dated Oct. 4, 2020 (part of the headline is cut off).

## What reports confirm

- On Oct. 4, 2020, Yonhap News Agency reported that, citing 1974 East German diplomatic documents Seoul National University professor Sung Sang-hwan (성상환) discovered in the German Foreign Ministry's Political Archive, it was confirmed that Kim Il Sung (김일성) had told the incoming Soviet ambassador on Oct. 25 of that year, "The terrorist Mun Se-gwang (문세광) probably belongs to the leftist wing of Mindan," and "they are followers of Kim Dae-jung (김대중)" — an apparent attempt to shift responsibility for the shooting of Yuk Young-soo (육영수) onto Kim Dae-jung's camp (reprinted by Daehan Kyungjae (대한경제) and others).
- In September 2026, over the film "Assassin(s)" (directed by Heo Jin-ho (허진호)), which deals with the shooting of Yuk Young-soo, People Power Party lawmakers Joo Jin-woo (주진우) and Yoo Young-ha (유영하) and others reportedly criticized the film for distorting history by portraying former President Park Chung-hee (박정희), rather than North Korea, as the mastermind, and demanded that those responsible be punished.

## What we left out of the summary

The diplomatic document records a remark Kim Il Sung made to the Soviet ambassador; it does not itself constitute a historical judgment on who was behind Mun Se-gwang. The filmmakers' position was not covered in the reports we found this time.
