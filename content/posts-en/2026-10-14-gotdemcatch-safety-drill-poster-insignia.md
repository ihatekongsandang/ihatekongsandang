## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says that a police officer illustrated on promotional materials for the government-wide "Safe Korea disaster response drill" starting next week wore star-shaped insignia not used on South Korean police uniforms, resembling those of the Chinese People's Liberation Army or Vietnamese public security; that the materials were used on banners and online nationwide; and that after KBS began reporting, the interior ministry sent local governments an official letter to scrap them.
- It asks who made the image and what review it went through, saying the government could not even check uniforms and insignia on materials for a drill meant to keep people safe.

## What reports confirm

- On Oct. 14, KBS reported that a police officer on banners promoting the Safe Korea drill has stars on the shoulders, whereas South Korean police insignia consist of mugunghwa (rose of Sharon) flowers or buds, and star-shaped insignia are found on those of the Chinese People's Liberation Army or Vietnamese public security. It also said the emblem on a firefighter's helmet differs from the National Fire Agency's official logo.
- The interior ministry, which made and distributed the materials, reportedly created them using generative AI and did not check them properly. A ministry official was quoted as saying "mistakes like that happened because we did it with AI, and we have taken steps to scrap it as soon as possible," and the ministry reportedly sent local governments a letter ordering the materials scrapped after KBS began reporting.

## What we left out of the summary

- Reports attribute the cause to generative AI production and insufficient checking; there is no evidence the Chinese military-style insignia were included deliberately. We could not confirm which contractor or official actually made the materials.
