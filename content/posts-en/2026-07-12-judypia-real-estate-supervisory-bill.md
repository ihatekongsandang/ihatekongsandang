This post is based on the original caption; the video content was not reviewed. The original was posted on July 12, 2026, and the bill was later withdrawn and reintroduced.

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- The Real Estate Supervisory Agency would be an independent law-enforcement agency directly under the prime minister, with special judicial police, constantly looking up all citizens' financial accounts, tax, immigration and health insurance information without a warrant.
- The burden of proof would shift to citizens, so that even money contributed by family members could be branded as illegal funds without documentation, while foreign speculative capital could not be traced, creating reverse discrimination.
- The majority party aims to pass it on its own within the year.

## What reports confirm

- Democratic Party Rep. Kim Hyun-jung (김현정) reportedly sponsored, on Feb. 10, 2026, a bill creating a "Real Estate Supervisory Agency" under the prime minister, under which the agency would directly investigate illegal real estate activity and oversee and coordinate the inquiries, investigations and sanctions of the Land Ministry, the National Tax Service, financial authorities and local governments.
- The original bill reportedly allowed the agency to demand attendance, statements and submission of materials, conduct on-site inspections and seize documents, and request transaction-report data, tax and administrative data, financial transaction information and loan status, while limiting the provision of financial transaction and credit information to the minimum necessary and requiring prior review by a real estate supervision council.
- On Sept. 23, reflecting concerns raised during legislative discussions, Rep. Kim reportedly withdrew the existing bill and again sponsored a new bill that strengthens controls on its powers, including **deleting the basis for demanding credit information and separating inquiries from criminal investigations**.

## What we left out of the summary

"Real-time constant surveillance without a warrant," "a staff of about 100," "deployment of special judicial police," "shifting the burden of proof" and "a push to pass it alone within the year" could not be confirmed from the reports we found this time. The bill is at the introduction stage and has not passed the National Assembly.
