The JCS interim findings released that evening are summarized in the [JCS interim findings post](/post/2026-09-28-pagememo-jcs-interim-result). Earlier developments (some may be available only in Korean): [eyewitness testimony](/post/2026-09-27-rekor-dmz-mine-testimony) · [questions about the delayed investigation](/post/2026-09-28-minkyuhan-dmz-delay-questions).

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It demands to know who ordered the phrase "suspected North Korean box mine" removed from the initial report, and why.

## What reports confirm

- At a National Assembly press conference on Sept. 28, Rep. Na Kyung-won reportedly said, "The initial military report clearly included the words 'suspected North Korean box mine,'" and urged, "Please make clear who had it deleted and for what reason."
- She reportedly argued that the chain of explosions proved the mines were buried and that it was clearly a North Korean box mine, and demanded a full investigation and accountability, saying the government knew North Korean forces might be laying mines south of the MDL but did nothing.
- At the time of the press conference, the military had not identified the explosive as a North Korean box mine; the JCS reportedly said there was testimony that the object looked like a box mine but was made of plastic, and that it would verify this through testimony and a site investigation.
- That evening, the JCS announced interim findings that "it is not our mine and very likely a North Korean mine."

## What we left out of the summary

- That the phrase was in the initial report and later deleted is Na's claim; neither the original report nor an official military explanation could be confirmed from reports.
