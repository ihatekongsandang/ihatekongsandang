The original post shares six screenshots of a Chosun Ilbo column with the line "Please, let there be no one who hasn't seen this." We summarize only the gist rather than reproducing the column, and used a card made by this site instead of the original images. What follows is **the columnist's account and assessment**.

## Gist of the column (the columnist's opinion)

- It says that after President Park Chung-hee lost First Lady Yuk Young-soo (육영수) to shots fired by Mun Se-gwang (문세광), a young Korean resident of Japan, at the Aug. 15, 1974 ceremony, Kim Young-kwang (김영광), then a Korea Central Intelligence Agency director, proposed a home-visit program for Chongryon-affiliated Korean residents, and that Park, at first furious, ordered a detailed plan a week later. Citing Kim Choong-sik's book "The Directors of Namsan," it quotes Park as saying, "Geun-hye was against it too, but I decided because I am the president."
- It says about 700 Chongryon-affiliated Korean residents of Japan visited their hometowns for Chuseok in September 1975 for the first time since liberation, that they wept when "The Unfilial Son Weeps" was sung at the Sept. 24 welcome ceremony at the National Theater, and that as thousands visited each year and changed nationality, Chongryon shrank from nearly 500,000 at its peak to about 20,000.
- The columnist criticizes the film "Assassin(s)," about the shooting of First Lady Yuk, for advancing a conspiracy theory that the presidential security service and the KCIA jointly carried out the shooting and for listing circumstances that cast suspicion on Park as the mastermind, and argues that inviting the "people behind" the wife's death and restoring their hometowns to them is a truth outside the film that "can only be called a 'great revenge.'"

## What we confirmed

- The column ran in Chosun Ilbo's [Taepyeongro] section under the name of editorial writer Kim Tae-hoon (김태훈) (Oct. 7 print edition).
- "Assassin(s)," directed by Hur Jin-ho (허진호) and based on the Aug. 15, 1974 shooting and the death of First Lady Yuk, was released on Sept. 23; it was reported to hint that the assassin had accomplices and that the shooting may have been staged to help the regime out of a crisis, sparking controversy over its historical interpretation and a backlash among conservatives (Weekly Kyunghyang).

## What we left out of the summary

- The historical account — Chongryon's size (500,000 → 20,000), the number of visitors (about 700) and remarks by figures of the time — is the columnist's account, which this post did not check against other records.
- That the film "advanced a conspiracy theory" is the columnist's assessment; this post did not watch the film to check. The film is a drama based on a real event.
