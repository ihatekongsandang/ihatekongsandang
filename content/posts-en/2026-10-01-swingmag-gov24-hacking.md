This post is based on the original caption and first slide. The first slide shows a photo of President Lee Jae-myung overlaid with the text "Government24 hacked / All citizens' personal data 'to China' / Fines paid with citizens' taxes." Because "all citizens' personal data to China" is not confirmed by reports, we used a card made by this site instead of the original image.

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says it has belatedly come to light that Government24 (정부24) was hacked and that resident registration numbers and account information were leaked.
- It says an attack on Aug. 17 targeting a security flaw in the children's portal stole 11 server memory dump files, and that the Ministry of the Interior and Safety estimated 36 resident registration numbers, 3 phone numbers and 55 Government24 IDs and passwords were leaked, but criticizes that the exact scale of damage had still not been determined a month later.
- It says the ministry had already been fined 273 million won plus an administrative fine over an earlier Government24 personal data leak, and relays online reactions that fines paid by a government body are effectively borne by taxpayers.
- It says victims of the 1365 Volunteer Portal leak grew from an initial 796 to 8,785, and that the children's portal posted only a "system maintenance" notice instead of an apology.
- The first slide carries the text "All citizens' personal data, 'to China.'"

## What reports confirm

- According to materials Rep. Jung Choon-saeng (정춘생) of the Rebuilding Korea Party, a member of the National Assembly's Public Administration and Security Committee, obtained from the Ministry of the Interior and Safety, an external attack targeting a security flaw in Government24's children's portal at around 5:29 p.m. on Aug. 17 reportedly took 11 memory dump files and other data from Government24 servers.
- In a report filed with the Personal Information Protection Commission the next day, the ministry reportedly estimated that 36 resident registration numbers, 3 phone numbers and 55 Government24 IDs and passwords were leaked, and the exact scale of damage had reportedly not been determined more than a month later. This was reportedly the first time Government24 personal data was leaked through an external hack.
- Rep. Jung reportedly pointed out that the children's portal posted only a "system maintenance (service suspended)" notice rather than notice of the leak or an apology, and that no leak notice appeared among Government24's announcements.
- Victims of the 1365 Volunteer Portal leak that occurred on May 20 reportedly numbered 8,785, more than 11 times the 796 first reported to the Personal Information Protection Commission.
- In May 2026, the Personal Information Protection Commission reportedly imposed a 273 million won penalty surcharge and a 7.5 million won administrative fine on the ministry over an earlier Government24 incident in which the personal data of 1,233 people was exposed (civil documents shown to other users because of a source code error).

## What we left out of the summary

- **"All citizens' personal data, 'to China'"**: The reports we checked say nothing about who carried out the attack or about leaked data going to China. The reported estimated leak is 36 resident registration numbers and similar figures; we found no report of a leak on the scale of "all citizens."
- **"Fines paid with citizens' taxes"**: The original caption presents this as an online reaction. Reports did not confirm from which budget the ministry's penalty was actually paid. The 273 million won penalty was imposed over the earlier incident, not the August hack.
- The original first slide's photo of the president was not reflected in the summary, as reports did not explain any connection between him and the hack.
