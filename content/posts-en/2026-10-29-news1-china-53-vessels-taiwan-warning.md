This post is based on news reports. Taiwan's statements are as relayed by South Korean outlets citing Taiwan's Central News Agency (CNA) and others.

## What reports confirm

- Joseph Wu (우자오셰), secretary-general of the National Security Council at Taiwan's Presidential Office, posted a map showing the locations of 53 Chinese vessels on X on Oct. 29 and said "more than 50 Chinese warships and official vessels are operating across a wide area from southern Japan to the waters off Taiwan and the Philippines, posing an obvious threat," according to reports citing CNA.
- Seoul Shinmun reported that, according to the released material, 34 of the 50-plus vessels were navy ships led by the aircraft carrier Liaoning; the Liaoning group was east of the Philippines and the remaining 20 or so were off eastern and southeastern Taiwan and near southern Japan.
- Japan's Kyodo News and others reportedly said three Chinese warships and one coast guard vessel passed through the Osumi Strait near Kyushu into the Pacific.
- Taiwan's defense minister Wellington Koo (구리슝) reportedly said the military was "closely monitoring the People's Liberation Army's activities and responding appropriately."

## What we left out of the summary

- The view that the maneuvers are a deliberate show of force aimed at checking both the U.S.-Japan alliance and Taiwan is reported in the article as "some analysts' view" and was left out of the factual summary. The Chinese government's official position on these maneuvers did not appear in the South Korean reports we checked, so we did not state it.
