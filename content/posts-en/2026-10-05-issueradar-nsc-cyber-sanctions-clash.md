## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says that at the Oct. 1 National Security Council standing committee meeting, the foreign ministry proposed adding individuals and entities involved in North Korean hacking, crypto-asset theft and overseas IT workers to Korea's unilateral sanctions list, and that Unification Minister Chung Dong-young (정동영) strongly objected, questioning whether it was right to bring up cyber sanctions unrelated to the mine incident now, leading to raised voices with Foreign Minister Cho Hyun (조현).
- It summarizes that the foreign affairs and defense officials see a need to respond because soldiers were injured, while the unification ministry argues for caution because hard-line responses such as sanctions or resuming loudspeaker broadcasts could raise tensions.
- The first frame reads "Don't sanction North Korean hacking / Chung Dong-young opposes sanctions unrelated to the mine / Do we have to worry about North Korea's feelings too?"

## What reports confirm

- At the Oct. 1 NSC standing committee meeting, Foreign Minister Cho reportedly proposed, as security measures toward North Korea including the response to the DMZ mine blast, designating individuals and entities involved in North Korean hacking, crypto-asset theft and overseas IT worker activities for unilateral sanctions. Designation would reportedly freeze their domestic assets and restrict financial transactions with South Korean nationals.
- Unification Minister Chung reportedly objected strongly, saying in effect that it was not directly related to the mine incident and was beside the point, and reportedly raised his voice with Minister Cho while citing President Lee's reaffirmation that same day, in his Armed Forces Day speech, of his stance on lowering military tensions. One ruling-camp source reportedly said Chung "reacted with something close to fury."
- A foreign ministry official reportedly said on Oct. 5 that it is "at the internal review stage, and nothing has been confirmed," while explaining that North Korea's illicit cyber activities are a major funding source for its nuclear and missile programs and that the government is responding in cooperation with the international community.
- Resuming loudspeaker broadcasts toward the North reportedly is not under consideration, while the government is reportedly discussing details of demanding an apology from North Korea, a demonstration of the military's advanced capabilities and the need for military talks to resolve the mismatch over the MDL baseline.
- At its final investigation briefing on Oct. 5, the JCS reportedly said it was "not a drifted mine but a typical North Korean mine that was planted," and that the North Korean minefield south of the MDL was removed that day.

## What we left out of the summary

- **"Don't sanction North Korean hacking"**: No report says Minister Chung said this. The reported reasons for his opposition were the lack of a direct link to the mine incident, its distance from the core issue and the government's tension-reduction stance.
- **"Do we have to worry about North Korea's feelings too?"** is the poster's assessment.
- What was said at the NSC meeting was reported through accounts from ruling-camp sources and others; the only official government statement is the foreign ministry's "under internal review."
