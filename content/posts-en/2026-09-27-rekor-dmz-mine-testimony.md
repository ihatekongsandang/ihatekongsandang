This post is based on the original caption; the card image content was not reviewed. The original caption cites Chosun Ilbo and Munhwa Ilbo reports and a Joint Chiefs of Staff announcement as its sources.

## What reports confirm

- According to a Sept. 28 Chosun Ilbo report, troops at the site of the Sept. 21 blast, suspected to be a mine explosion in a front-line Army 25th Division unit in the DMZ, testified from right after the blast to the effect that they had seen a North Korean box mine, and voices from front-line units reportedly called for a swift site investigation. This was reportedly the first time such direct eyewitness testimony had come to light.
- The military reportedly did not begin an investigation immediately after the accident, citing the injured troops' trauma and safety, and conducted a joint site investigation with the UN Command six days later, on the 27th.
- In the Sept. 27 investigation, mine detectors were reportedly used to search the access route from around 9 a.m. until sunset, but the team could not reach the blast point; the JCS reportedly said it ended the operation and would resume it later because "the unconfirmed minefield poses high risk, and personnel fatigue was also extreme."
- The JCS had reportedly said earlier, on the 25th, that it had "been thoroughly checking the situation based on testimony from the troops involved in the operation from right after it occurred" and that it had asked the National Forensic Service on the 22nd to analyze the composition of the injured troops' gear and fragments, countering that claims of a deliberate delay were a distortion of fact.
- Opposition figures reportedly criticized the delay, suggesting it was put off "for fear of upsetting North Korea."

## What we left out of the summary

Whether the cause of the blast was a North Korean box mine is, at this stage, based on reported troop testimony and circumstantial analysis; the military's official investigation result had not yet come out. Opposition criticism and the JCS's explanation for the delay diverge.

## Later developments

On the evening of Sept. 28, 2026, the JCS announced interim findings saying "it is not our mine and very likely a North Korean mine" (without yet concluding whether it was recently laid or displaced). Details: [JCS interim findings](/post/2026-09-28-pagememo-jcs-interim-result).
