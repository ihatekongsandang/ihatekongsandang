## What reports confirm

- According to material People Power Party Rep. Ko Dong-jin (고동진) obtained from the Ministry of Unification on Sept. 8, 2026, the ministry's Center for North Korean Human Rights Records has reportedly redesignated the report on the results of its "survey on the state of North Korean human rights" as a Level 3 secret since 2025 and has not disclosed it. Level 3 secrets are designated for matters whose leakage could harm national security.
- The North Korean human rights report was reportedly published without public release in 2018–2022 under the Moon Jae-in (문재인) administration, then switched to public release in 2023 and 2024 under the Yoon Suk Yeol (윤석열) administration, with an English edition also produced.
- Rep. Ko reportedly demanded a review of the arbitrary secrecy designation, saying, "It is hard to understand why the Unification Ministry, while saying it will open up the Rodong Sinmun and North Korean TV in the name of the public's right to know, keeps trying to hide the report that actually documents the human rights conditions of North Korean residents."

## What we left out of the summary

We could not confirm from the reports we found this time the original's figure of "testimony from 649 North Korean defectors," or its statement that the ministry cited "insufficient testimony due to fewer defectors" and "little effect from an aggressive disclosure approach" as reasons for nondisclosure.
