This post is based on the original caption posted on Sept. 13, 2026; the video content was not reviewed. The original thumbnail labels the video's source as a "National Assembly press conference."

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It relays that Rep. Lee Jin-sook (이진숙) demanded that the state explain to the public whom it recognized as May 18 merit holders, for what achievements and through what procedures.
- It conveys Rep. Lee's point that the reason people who were not at the scene during May 18 were included on the list of merit holders should also be explained, and criticizes passing a resolution urging her expulsion before answering the demand for an explanation as ignoring the heart of the matter.
- It says that since taxpayers' money is involved, the selection criteria, records of merit and review procedures should be disclosed, and expresses support for Rep. Lee.

## What reports confirm

- Rep. Lee reportedly held a May 18-related forum at the National Assembly Library on Aug. 13, where claims at odds with established historical assessments were made, such as describing May 18 as "a ten-day disturbance," sparking a controversy over "disparaging May 18."
- On Aug. 26, the National Assembly reportedly passed a resolution urging Rep. Lee's expulsion, with 157 of the 159 members present voting in favor, and this was reportedly the first time in constitutional history that a resolution urging a lawmaker's expulsion passed a plenary session. Most People Power Party members did not take part in the vote.
- On Aug. 31, Rep. Lee reportedly said she would push to amend the law to create a basis for disclosing the names and records of May 18 merit holders and to make review by the Ministry of Patriots and Veterans Affairs mandatory when merit holders are registered.
- At a National Assembly press conference on Sept. 8, Rep. Lee reportedly said, "The point is transparency," arguing that the public should know for what achievements merit holders receive benefits such as bonus points on civil service exams, and countered that the forum was held on the premise that May 18 was a democratization movement and that "they are taking issue with some expressions to claim it disparaged May 18."

## What we left out of the summary

- The statement that "people who were not at the scene were included on the list of merit holders" was relayed only as Rep. Lee's claim; we could not confirm from reports specifically who this refers to, so we did not present it as fact.
