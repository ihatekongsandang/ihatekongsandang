This post is based on the text in the original image and the caption ("Do people really have to get hurt before they come to their senses?"). Related posts (some may be available only in Korean): [Kim Moon-soo (김문수) criticizes the "abolition of the Prosecutors' Office"](/post/2026-09-21-kimmoonsoo-prosecution-abolition) · [Revisiting the North Korean directive on a "first-stage goal of prosecution reform"](/post/2026-08-03-pagememo-nk-prosecution-reform-directive).

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- Saying, "If it becomes a prosecutors' republic, politicians get hurt; if it becomes a police republic, ordinary people get hurt," it expresses concern about cutting the prosecution's powers and concentrating investigative authority in the police.

## What reports confirm

- An amendment to the Criminal Procedure Act abolishing prosecutors' direct and supplementary investigation authority reportedly passed a plenary session of the National Assembly on July 31, 2026, and after the amendment, prosecutors can reportedly request supplementary investigations from judicial police officers. This is tied to the abolition of the Prosecutors' Office on Oct. 2 and the launch of the Public Prosecution Agency (공소청) and the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency (중대범죄수사청).
- Former intelligence officials and others reportedly expressed concern that, as the police take over investigative authority under the Criminal Procedure Act amendment, the police are struggling internally to absorb the work.

## What we left out of the summary

- The original is an expression of opinion; neither the original nor the reports we checked contain specific cases or statistics showing that harm to ordinary people has increased under a "police republic."
