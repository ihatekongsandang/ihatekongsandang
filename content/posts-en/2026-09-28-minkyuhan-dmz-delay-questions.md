Other posts on this issue (some may be available only in Korean): [report on troops' eyewitness testimony](/post/2026-09-27-rekor-dmz-mine-testimony) · [JCS testimony about an object resembling a box mine](/post/2026-09-28-facteye-dmz-mine-similar-object).

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It argues that **if it is confirmed** that the presidential office and the military knew of the possibility North Korea was responsible but blocked disclosure of the testimony, delayed the investigation, revised reports and ordered silence because of the burden of the UN address and overseas trips, this would not be a mere initial-response failure but would raise suspicions of downplaying and a cover-up.

## What reports confirm

- Troops at the scene reportedly testified, from right after the Sept. 21 blast, to the effect that they had "seen a North Korean army box mine." At a Sept. 28 briefing, the JCS reportedly said it had received testimony describing a shape similar to a box mine but that it had not been confirmed to be an actual box mine.
- The military reportedly did not begin an investigation immediately after the accident, citing the injured troops' trauma and safety, and began a site investigation with the UN Command six days later, on the 27th.
- On the 25th, the JCS reportedly countered that the claim of a deliberate delay was a distortion of fact, saying it had "been thoroughly checking the situation based on testimony from the troops involved in the operation from right after it occurred."
- President Lee Jae-myung reportedly attended the UN General Assembly (in New York) and made a state visit to Mexico during this period.

## What we left out of the summary

The claims that testimony disclosure was blocked, the investigation was deliberately delayed, reports were revised and silence was ordered are raised in the original itself only as a conditional ("if confirmed"), and we could not find reports supporting them.
