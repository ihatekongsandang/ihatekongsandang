This post is based on the original caption. The original is a multi-slide card post; Instagram shows it as posted on Nov. 2 (U.S. time), but the Cabinet meeting the caption calls "today" was held on Nov. 3 Korea time, so we used that date.

## What the original post claims (the poster's opinion)

- It says President Lee Jae-myung told a Cabinet meeting "today" to discuss limiting teenagers' social media use "carefully and quickly," and that the Korea Media and Communications Commission aims to finish by the first half of next year. It adds that at the same meeting he called fake news a problem that "shakes the foundations of society" and took issue with reporting that relies on quotation marks.
- It says a "law to eradicate false and manipulated information" passed late last year under the ruling party's lead without the opposition, so that media outlets and YouTubers found to spread such information must pay up to five times the damage. It says even the civic group People's Solidarity for Participatory Democracy (참여연대) called it inappropriate for the state to judge what is false.
- It says North Korea blocks the internet and has publicly executed people for sharing South Korean songs and films, citing the Unification Ministry's North Korean human rights report.
- It calls all this "communization work" and "the start of communization," asks who will be next after teenagers, and urges people to stop SNS regulation.

## What reports confirm

- At a Cabinet meeting at Cheong Wa Dae on Nov. 3, President Lee reportedly asked Kim Jong-cheol (김종철), chairman of the Korea Media and Communications Commission, how discussions on limiting teenagers' social media use were going and said, "Discuss it carefully and quickly." He reportedly mentioned debates in Europe, Australia and Canada and polls in which "close to 80%" supported limits.
- Kim reportedly answered that a cautious approach was needed, citing the failure of the game "shutdown" rule, and said the plan was to finish after public deliberation in the first half of next year. The specific level and method of any limit have not been decided, reports said.
- At the same meeting, Lee reportedly said fake news "has become a problem that harms the basic order of the state" and one that "shakes the foundations of society," and, referring to so-called quotation-mark reporting, said, "Don't they think everything is excused if they put it in quotation marks?" He also reportedly said fact-checking and related education should be expanded.
- An amendment to the Information and Communications Network Act — dubbed the "law to eradicate false and manipulated information" — that lets courts impose punitive damages of up to five times the loss on media outlets, YouTubers and others whose false or manipulated information causes harm passed the National Assembly on Dec. 24, 2025, under the ruling bloc's lead with the People Power Party not voting, and was approved by the Cabinet on Dec. 30, reports said.
- People's Solidarity for Participatory Democracy reportedly said "the very idea that the state will step in to judge whether information is true and block its spread is inappropriate," and asked the president to veto the bill.
- The Unification Ministry's North Korean human rights report, first made public in March 2023, reportedly contains defectors' testimony that people were publicly executed for reasons such as distributing South Korean dramas.

## What we left out of the summary

- The order confirmed by reports concerns limits on social media use by teenagers. The post's opening phrase, "a quick review of SNS regulation," leaves out that the target is teenagers, and we found no report of plans to regulate adults' social media.
- "Communization work," "the start of communization," "it cannot be undone," and the forecast that others will be targeted after teenagers are the poster's assessments. We found no reports supporting them.
- We did not include the claim that the prime minister said "fake news is not free speech but a clear crime" and ordered police to crack down on "pseudo-media," because we could not find a report of that remark.
- The point that age checks would require an identification system is the poster's inference; reports say the method of any limit has not been decided.
- The North Korean human rights report content comes from reports on a report compiling defectors' testimony; we did not check the provisions of the Reactionary Ideology and Culture Rejection Act that the post also cites.
